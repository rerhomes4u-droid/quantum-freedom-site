Practice Handling Curveballs

🌟 Key Mindset Reminders:

1. You are the WRONGED PARTY. They must prove their case.

2. Stay calm, speak slowly, and pause before answering.

3. Always return to your core points: NO EVIDENCE, NO COURT ORDER, NO JURISDICTION.

4. Use the phrase "For the record..." to document important points.

Potential Curveball #1: The Judge Interrupts or Rushes You

Judge says: "Ms. Herrera, we don't have time for all this. Get to the point."

Your response (calmly): "Your Honor, I understand the court's time is limited. However, the unlawful separation from my son for nearly two months is an emergency that requires a complete record. For the record, I need to establish that there is no valid court order authorizing this deprivation of my parental rights. May I proceed with this fundamental jurisdictional question?"

Why this works: You acknowledge their concern but reframe it as a NECESSARY discussion about jurisdiction, which judges cannot easily dismiss.

Potential Curveball #2: They Cite "DSS Authority"

Opposing Attorney says: "DSS has statutory authority to protect children, and their investigation found credible evidence of risk."

Your response (firmly): "For the record, DSS never interviewed me during their so-called investigation. Authority without due process is tyranny. I demand to see the evidence that justifies separating a breastfeeding mother from her infant for two months. Please present it now. And while you're at it, please show me where in any statute I waived my fundamental, pre-existing rights as a mother."

Why this works: You expose their lack of due process and put the burden back on them to produce evidence they likely don't have.

Potential Curveball #3: They Claim "Emergency" or "Imminent Danger"

Social Worker says: "We had to act quickly to protect the child from imminent danger."

Your response (calmly, with facts): "For the record, there was no emergency. My child was safely in another room during an adult disagreement. Where is the evidence of imminent danger? If there was truly an emergency two months ago, why hasn't there been a hearing with actual evidence presented? This 'emergency' has become a permanent, unlawful seizure without due process."

Why this works: You challenge their timeline - emergencies require immediate judicial review, not two months of separation without a hearing.

Potential Curveball #4: The Judge Refuses to Hear Your Questions About Authority

Judge says: "Ms. Herrera, I don't need to show you my oath of office. We're here to discuss the DSS finding."

Your response (respectfully but firmly): "Your Honor, with all due respect, jurisdiction is the first question in any legal proceeding. For the record, I am appearing under duress and coercion because my child has been taken without a court order. Before I can participate in any discussion about allegations, I need to know this court has proper authority. The circuit clerk's office could not produce your Oath of Office or Surety Bond. Can you direct me to where these are filed so I can verify this court's jurisdiction over my fundamental rights?"

Why this works: You're not being difficult - you're asking a legitimate procedural question that goes to the heart of whether they have authority over you.

Potential Curveball #5: They Try to Focus Only on the "Incident"

Attorney says: "Let's talk about what happened on October 18th. The father says you were violent."

Your response (redirecting): "I've addressed that in my sworn affidavit which they refused to accept. But more importantly, even if we discuss that incident, it doesn't justify two months of separation without a hearing. Where is the court order? Where is the evidence? For the record, my child was safe in another room. The real issue is the unlawful deprivation of rights without due process that's happening RIGHT NOW."

Why this works: You don't get dragged into their narrative. You keep the focus on the CURRENT injustice - the unlawful separation.

How to Read Your Affidavit Into The Record

📜 Critical Instructions for Reading Into Record:

Since they refused to accept your affidavit, you MUST read it into the official court record. Follow these steps:

  1. Wait for your turn to speak - don't interrupt
  2. Begin with: "Your Honor, for the record, I need to read my sworn affidavit into the official record since the clerk's office refused to accept it for filing."
  3. If the judge objects: "Your Honor, this affidavit contains critical facts about the lack of investigation and due process. Since it won't be in the written record, I need to ensure these facts are preserved for appeal."
  4. Read SLOWLY and CLEARLY - the court reporter needs to capture every word
  5. Pause briefly between paragraphs
  6. End with: "This concludes my sworn affidavit, which I hereby enter into the record."

Practice Reading This Key Section:

"For the record, I, Carolina Herrera, being duly sworn, state under oath:

At no time during the DSS investigation was I interviewed, contacted, or asked to provide my version of events. DSS relied solely on statements made by the child's father, with no balanced or neutral investigation.

On October 18, my child was never in danger. My child was inside the bathtub, playing calmly, in a controlled and safe environment, physically distant from any adult disagreement.

Most critically, there exists NO COURT ORDER from any jurisdiction that terminates my parental rights or restricts my contact with my son. The continued separation is therefore unlawful.

For over 45 days, I have been denied all contact with my breastfeeding child, causing severe medical and emotional harm to both of us, without any judicial authorization.

I make this statement under penalty of perjury, and demand the immediate restoration of my son based on the complete absence of legal authority for his continued separation from me."

💡 Pro Tip: The Magic Phrase

Use "For the record..." whenever you need to:

  • Document a fact the judge should know
  • Preserve an objection for appeal
  • State that you're being denied due process
  • Note that evidence is being ignored

This phrase signals to the court reporter: "THIS MUST BE WRITTEN DOWN."
