A Voice Silenced

Who Shoshana Strook was

Shoshana Strook was an Israeli activist and psychology graduate who became internationally known in 2025 after publicly accusing her parents and one of her brothers of systematic sexual, physical, and psychological abuse spanning from early childhood. Her mother, Orit Strook, is Israel's Minister of Settlements and National Missions, a powerful figure in the far-right religious Zionist movement[citation:1][citation:17].

Shoshana was one of eleven children raised in an environment of radical religious Zionism, residing largely in settlements in the occupied West Bank, particularly Hebron[citation:17][citation:18]. She held a master's degree in psychology and had aspirations to help other survivors of ritualistic abuse. She was found dead in a rented cabin in Amirim, northern Israel, on March 15, 2026, at the age of 34[citation:1][citation:2].

⚠️ The Circumstances

Shoshana was found dead in a chalet she had rented in the settlement of Amirim, Upper Galilee, on March 15, 2026. Israeli authorities initially suggested suicide, with the volunteer organization ZAKA stating that autopsy results "do not indicate suspicions of criminal activity"[citation:2]. However, Shoshana had explicitly stated months before her death that she had no suicidal tendencies and that if she were found dead, specific parties should bear responsibility[citation:15]. Her friend Yael Shitrit stated: "She didn't want to die. She wanted a home and security. If this nation doesn't wake up, we're next"[citation:1].

The Allegations

What Shoshana exposed

👶 Ritual Abuse from Age 2.5

Shoshana alleged that from the age of two and a half, her parents took her to pedophile ceremonies where she was "programmed and trained through drugs, hypnosis and sexual abuse"[citation:1][citation:12]. She described being forced to participate in ritual ceremonies involving the use of drugs and hypnosis, and claimed her personality was fractured into "different parts, different identities" as a result[citation:12].

💰 Trafficked at 13

She claimed that from the age of 13, her father exposed her to prostitution in Tel Aviv, making money from her rape[citation:2][citation:5]. She stated: "From the age of 13, my father began marketing me in Tel Aviv. He made money from me. This abuse continued into quite advanced ages"[citation:12].

🎥 Documented Abuse

Shoshana alleged that her mother actively participated in and documented the abuse, with sexual assaults filmed under the guise of religious rituals[citation:10]. She stated: "I was sexually assaulted by my father and mother, and the sexual assault I experienced was filmed under the pretext of religious rituals"[citation:10].

🔄 Continued Abuse as Adult

Even after going public, Shoshana claimed her father continued to harm her. In a later video, she stated: "Even though I had already exposed this and told my story, my father continues to harm me. He came to the apartment I rented and lived in alone and abused me again. He brought someone else with him"[citation:12]. She alleged he was training the person to be her "new handler" as part of a system passed down through generations[citation:12].

🛡️ Threats and Homelessness

After the last alleged assault, Shoshana said she had to leave her apartment and had no home: "Since the last assault, I left my apartment, I currently have no home. I found some good people who are hosting me for a few days, but I have no means of financial support. Because I struggle with Dissociative Identity Disorder, I am in a very difficult mental state and I am truly facing the danger of being homeless"[citation:12].

⚖️ Complaint Filed in Italy

Fearing that her mother's political power would prevent a fair investigation in Israel, Shoshana traveled to Italy in late 2025 and filed a formal criminal complaint with Italian authorities, seeking international protection and "legal asylum" in a neutral country[citation:17][citation:18].

The Strook Family

Political power and past convictions

👩‍💼 Orit Strook (Mother)

Israel's Minister of Settlements and National Missions, a leader in the far-right Religious Zionist Party. Known for her extremist views and vocal support of illegal Israeli settlements in the occupied Palestinian territories[citation:13]. She has also promoted unfounded claims about Hamas sexual assaults on October 7[citation:13]. After Shoshana's death, Orit posted on Facebook: "It is with a broken heart that we announce the passing of our beloved daughter, Shoshana"[citation:1].

🕍 Avraham Strook (Father)

A rabbi who holds a religious position in the Hebron community[citation:15]. Shoshana alleged he was the primary perpetrator of the abuse and trafficked her to Tel Aviv[citation:2].

⚔️ Zviki Strook (Brother)

Convicted in 2007 for the kidnapping, torture, and assault of a Palestinian teenager. He was sentenced to 30 months in prison but served only nine months[citation:3][citation:13]. Orit Strook defended her son after his conviction, stating: "Unlike the court, which preferred to believe the Arab witnesses, we are sure of Zvi's innocence and are hurting from the success of his haters"[citation:13].

🏠 Family Background

Shoshana was one of eleven children raised in a radical religious Zionist environment. The family lived for extended periods in settlements in the West Bank, particularly Hebron[citation:17][citation:18]. The family has been at the center of media and legal attention multiple times, including Zviki's 2007 conviction[citation:17].

🔍 Political Context

Orit Strook's position as a government minister created significant concerns about whether a fair investigation into Shoshana's allegations could be conducted. Shoshana explicitly stated she fled to Italy because she feared her mother's political power would prevent justice in Israel[citation:5][citation:18]. The gag order imposed on Israeli media further complicated transparency[citation:3].

🔇

The Gag Order

Silencing the truth in Israel

A gag order was imposed by Israeli judicial authorities prohibiting local media from reporting on the case and from naming the minister involved. This restriction was heavily criticized by civil rights and feminist organizations as a potential institutional conflict of interest[citation:17].

🚫 Media Blackout

When Shoshana's first disclosure video was released, Israeli media was subject to a gag order prohibiting any reporting on the details of the case or its connection to Minister Strook[citation:12]. The order remained in place for months, preventing the Israeli public from learning about the allegations through domestic channels.

⚖️ Court Decision

A month after Shoshana's death, the court finally permitted publication of the fact that in 2025, police had investigated a complaint that Shoshana had been sexually assaulted[citation:7]. Judge Menachem Mizrahi quoted the police assessment: "the reasonable suspicion was extremely thin, if not lower"[citation:7]. The decision noted that the police did not find grounds to investigate her parents.

🗣️ International Outcry

International media outlets, particularly in Turkey, the Arab world, and France, covered the story extensively while Israeli media remained largely silent[citation:3][citation:5][citation:12]. This disparity highlighted the effectiveness of the gag order in containing the story within Israel.

👩‍⚖️ Women's Organizations Respond

Bonot Alternativa, a women's organization, urged the state to act: "The state's duty is now clear: to investigate, examine and uncover the truth"[citation:1]. Women's organizations in Israel called on the government to conduct a broader investigation, stressing that police failed to protect Shoshana properly[citation:1][citation:14].
