The Method — Four Steps

Every claim you encounter—from any source, in any situation—you handle in four steps. This is the method. It is the whole manual. Everything else is an application of it.

1. Name the Specific

Not "the system is corrupt." The specific: the specific document, the specific court case, the specific date, the specific person, the specific dollar amount. If you cannot name the specific, you do not have a claim. You have a feeling.

2. Find the Corroboration

What does the record actually show? Cite the source. If the source is contested, say so. If the source is anonymous, say so. If the source is a screenshot with no origin, say so. The record is the standard. Not the vibe. Not the algorithm. Not the influencer.

3. Mark the Gap

What is unknown? What has not been tested in court? What has not been independently verified? Say it plainly. The gap is not a weakness. The gap is the truth. Every honest claim has a gap. The dishonest claim pretends it does not.

4. Refuse to Overclaim

If the record does not support a claim, say so. Do not soften it. Do not hedge it into meaninglessness. Say: "The record I can find does not support this. Here is what it does support." That is the method. That is the work.

Why this matters

The method is not a weapon against the other side. It is a weapon against your own certainty. The person who runs the method on the claim he wants to be true is the person who cannot be captured by it. The person who only runs it on the enemy is a mirror image of the enemy.

Report — The Protocol

If you find evidence of trafficking, abuse, or exploitation, here is the protocol. Do not investigate alone. Do not confront. Do not share it publicly if it contains victim information. Report it.

📢 The Reporting Protocol

1

Document everything. Take screenshots. Save URLs. Note the dates and times. Do not alter the evidence.

2

Report to NCMEC. The National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. Report online or call 1-800-843-5678. Available 24/7.

report.cybertip.org

3

Report to the FBI. Submit a tip online or call 1-800-CALL-FBI. For emergencies, call 911.

tips.fbi.gov

4

Report to the platform. Every platform has a reporting mechanism. Use it. Keep a record of what you reported and when.

5

Do not investigate alone. If you find something, report it. Do not confront the person. Do not try to rescue the victim yourself. The authorities are the ones with the training and the legal authority. Your job is to document and report.
