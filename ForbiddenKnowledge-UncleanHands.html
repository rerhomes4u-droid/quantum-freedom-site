THE DOCTRINE

THE DOCTRINE

The unclean hands doctrine is one of the oldest principles in equity. It says: a party who comes to court with fraud, deceit, or bad faith cannot ask the court for relief.

If the other side has lied, cheated, or stolen to get where they are, they have no right to ask a judge to help them. Their hands are dirty. They must clean them before they can ask for anything.

"Fraud vitiates everything." — Harron v. Dyslin, 279 S.C. 475 (S.C. App. 1983)

▣ WHAT IT MEANS
THE RULE
A party who has acted in bad faith, fraudulently, or dishonestly in connection with the subject matter of the litigation cannot obtain equitable relief. The court will not help someone whose hands are unclean.
WHY IT MATTERS
If the other side has lied to the court, lied to the police, or lied to the agency, they cannot ask the court to trust them. The doctrine says: "You lied. You cheated. You stole. You don't get to ask for anything."
THE EFFECT
In some cases, the doctrine can dismiss a party's entire claim. In other cases, it can bar a party from testifying. In every case, it is a tool that forces the other side to account for their conduct.
▣ THE CASE LAW
Harron v. Dyslin, 279 S.C. 475 (S.C. App. 1983): "Fraud vitiates everything."
Ex parte Weeks, 82 S.C. 210 (1909): "Fraud vitiates all proceedings."
Floyd v. Floyd, 366 S.C. 369 (S.C. App. 2005): The unclean hands doctrine prevents a party from recovering where that party has acted fraudulently, inequitably, or in bad faith in the matter in litigation.
Parker v. Parker, 346 S.C. 562 (S.C. App. 2001): Unclean hands is an affirmative defense that must be pled and proven.
▣ THE STRATEGY
STEP 1
Identify the bad acts. What has the other side done? Lies to the court? Lies to the police? Lies to the agency? Fraud? Misrepresentation? Concealment of evidence? Identify each one.
STEP 2
Document the bad acts. Collect the evidence. The police report that contradicts their statement. The text message that proves they lied. The document that shows they committed fraud. You need proof, not just allegations.
STEP 3
Draft the notice. State each bad act. State the evidence that proves it. State the doctrine. State the consequence: the other side cannot obtain equitable relief.
STEP 4
File the notice. Serve it on the other side. File it with the court. Put it in the record. Now the court knows what the other side has done.
STEP 5
Argue it at the hearing. If the other side asks the court for anything, remind the court that they have unclean hands. The doctrine is a bar. They cannot obtain relief.

▣ THE PRO TIPS
TIP 1
Unclean hands must be pled. You cannot just say it at the hearing. You must file the notice. You must put it in the record. You must serve it on the other side.
TIP 2
Unclean hands must be proven. You cannot just allege it. You need evidence. The police report. The text message. The document. The testimony. Proof, not just accusation.
TIP 3
Unclean hands is a bar, not a weapon. It doesn't give you affirmative relief. It prevents the other side from getting relief. It is a shield, not a sword.
TIP 4
Be careful. If you have unclean hands too, the doctrine can be used against you. Before you file the notice, make sure your hands are clean. If they are not, clean them first.
TIP 5
Combine it with other tools. Unclean hands works well with a Motion to Compel, Requests for Admission, and Negative Averments. Each tool supports the others. Together, they build the case.
