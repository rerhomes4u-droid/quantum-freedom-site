✉️ The Three‑Letter Process – Build Evidence, Then Sue

Letter #1 (Initial dispute): Send via certified mail. Identify inaccuracies on your credit report. Be polite, factual. “This item is incorrect. Please investigate and remove.”
Letter #2 (Escalate): Cite statutes (15 USC §1681, §1692). Point out that they are violating the law. Demand correction.
Letter #3 (Notice of Intent to Sue): Timeline of events, copies of previous letters, green cards. State: “You have 10 days to cure or I will sue for $1,000 per violation plus actual damages.”
🎯 “The letter game is not to get them to fix it – it’s to BUILD EVIDENCE. Every ‘go pound sand’ response becomes Exhibit A. Every certified mail green card becomes proof of delivery. File the letters into the court. They hang themselves.”

📄 Filing the Federal Complaint – Three Required Documents

To start a federal lawsuit, you need: 1. Complaint (the allegations, causes of action, demand for relief).
2. Civil Cover Sheet (basic case information).
3. Proposed Summons (the form the clerk stamps to authorize service).

Where to file: Depending on your county (call clerk’s office or check online). There are only 3‑4 federal districts per state. Joey filed in the Southern District of Indiana.

Filing methods vary by court: Some allow email filing (Joey’s court) – he emailed everything and paid the next day. Others require hard copy with a check paperclipped (not stapled – they scan everything). Call the clerk and ask.

🔍 Discovery – The Gold Mine (ACDVs & Metadata)

“Discovery is where you get the evidence that wins cases. In credit cases, you want the ACDV (Automated Credit Dispute Verification) – the document the data furnisher sends to the credit reporting agency.”
📄 What an ACDV reveals:
- Who certified the data (name, phone number).
- The date of certification.
- The date of last payment reported.
- “By submitting this ACDV, you certify that you have reviewed all associated images and verify the accuracy of the data in compliance with all legal requirements.”
🔥 “In my case, the same guy, Anthony L., certified two different dates to two different credit bureaus five days apart. That’s NOT ‘maximum accuracy’ under 15 USC §1681. That’s a violation. Once we had that document, the case was over. They had no defense.”

How to get ACDVs: Send interrogatories and requests for production. Subpoena if necessary. Demand all communications between the data furnisher and the credit reporting agencies regarding your account.

📜 Subpoenas & Proposed Orders – You Write Everything

“Pro se litigants cannot issue subpoenas directly (attorneys can). You must go through the court. But that’s easy: you draft the subpoena, then you draft a proposed order granting the subpoena. File both. The judge signs. Then you can serve it.”
✍️ “In my case, Experian refused to produce documents. I wrote a motion to compel, attached a proposed order, and the judge signed it. Then Experian had to comply. They sent me 884 pages – including the ACDV that destroyed their case.”

Remember: The judge has hundreds of cases. If you write the order for them, they are much more likely to sign it. “You do their job for them. They love it.”

💥 Concrete Damages – The Defense’s First Attack

“The first thing defense attorneys say: ‘You have no concrete damages.’ You must prove actual injury.”

How Joey proved damages:
- Applied for a $2.1M business loan (reasonable for his existing business). Got denied. Kept the denial letter.
- Went to counseling for 10 months for emotional distress (anxiety from the credit inaccuracies). Signed HIPAA releases – gave the records to the defense.
- Sent interrogatories to the banker and the counselor. Their answers became evidence.

Statutory damages also available:
15 USC §1681n – Willful noncompliance: $100 to $1,000 per violation (actual damages also available).
15 USC §1692k – FDCPA: up to $1,000 per violation + actual damages + attorney fees (for attorney cases).
“I put $1,000 per violation in my settlement demand. Multiply by the number of inaccuracies, plus the loan denial, plus emotional distress. It adds up fast.”

🤝 Settlement Conference – The Judge as Referee

“A settlement conference is a zoom call where the magistrate judge puts each party in a separate virtual room. She goes back and forth negotiating. You must have someone on the call who can write a check (for the defense).”
🎯 “I went in with a $16.75 million demand. The defense came in at zero. After an hour and a half, the judge was exhausted. We didn’t settle that day – but the process forced them to take me seriously. We settled a week later for a very respectable amount.”

🛡️ The Two Defenses They Always Use – And How to Beat Them

1. “No concrete damages.”
→ Beat it by documenting loan denials, counseling costs, time spent, emotional distress. Get the denial letter. Get the counselor’s notes. Send interrogatories to third parties.

2. “No private right of action.”
→ This only applies to criminal statutes (Title 18). For civil statutes like 15 USC §1681 and §1692, there IS a private right of action. Quote the statute: “Any person who willfully fails to comply… is liable to that consumer.” (15 USC §1681n).
