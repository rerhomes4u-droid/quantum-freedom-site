💣 UCC §3-603 – Tender of Payment (The Top 3 Most Important Section)

“If tender of payment of an obligation to pay an instrument is made to a person entitled to enforce the instrument, and the tender is refused, there is discharge to the extent of the amount of the tender.”
🔥 “This is the nuclear option. You tender payment (performance) by endorsing the bill of exchange and sending it back. If they refuse it – they DISCHARGE your obligation. You don’t owe them anything. The debt is gone.”

How to use: When you receive a bill (credit card, utility, mortgage statement), endorse it “Without recourse / Pay to the order of [Your Name]” and send it back via certified mail. You have made tender of payment. If they refuse (and they usually will), under §3-603(b) your obligation is discharged to the full amount of the tender. Keep the green card as proof of delivery (acceptance by delivery).

🧠 UCC §3-603(c) – Deemed Tender (Intent is Performance)

“If presentment is required and the obligor is able and ready to pay on the due date at every place of payment stated in the instrument, the obligor is deemed to have made tender of payment on the due date to the person entitled to enforce the instrument.”
💡 “Even if you don’t physically send anything – just being able and ready to perform, and stating so, is deemed tender. This is huge. In court, say: ‘Your Honor, I am ready and able to perform right now. Give me an unconditional order to pay, and I will endorse it immediately.’ That alone triggers discharge protections.”

✅ UCC §3-602 – Payment (Performance) Discharges Obligation

“An instrument is paid to the extent payment is made by or on behalf of a party obligated to pay, to the person entitled to enforce the instrument. To the extent of the payment, the obligation is discharged.”

“Payment = performance = endorsement. When you endorse a bill and send it back, you have paid. Period. Their refusal doesn’t undo the fact that you made payment. The discharge happens upon refusal of tender, not upon acceptance.”

✂️ UCC §3-604 – Discharge by Cancellation or Renunciation

“A person entitled to enforce an instrument may discharge the obligation of a party by (1) intentional voluntary act such as surrender, destruction, mutilation, cancellation, striking out the party’s signature, or adding words indicating discharge; or (2) by agreeing not to sue or renouncing rights by a signed record.”

“You can discharge your own obligation just by writing ‘DISCHARGED’ on the instrument and signing it. Or by destroying the note. Or by sending a signed letter stating you renounce the right to sue. This is absolute power.”

⚡ “Signed with respect to an electronic record includes an electronic symbol, sound, or process – meaning ACQUIESCENCE counts as signature. If they don’t respond to your tender, their silence is acceptance under this provision.”

🛡️ UCC §3-601 – Discharge of Obligation

“The obligation of a party to pay the instrument is discharged as stated in this article or by an act or agreement which would discharge an obligation to pay money under a simple contract. Discharge is not effective against a person acquiring rights of a holder in due course without notice of the discharge.”

“If you discharge your debt, but then the instrument is transferred to a holder in due course who didn’t know about the discharge, they might still enforce it. That’s why you perfect your discharge with a court order or by physically cancelling the note.”

📝 Practical Example – Credit Card Bill (Tender Refusal = Discharge)

Step 1: Receive credit card bill (bill of exchange) for $1,000.
Step 2: Endorse the bill on the back: “Without recourse / Pay to the order of [Your Name]” and sign.
Step 3: Send it back to the credit card company via certified mail (adult signature).
Step 4: You have now made TENDER of payment under §3-603.
Step 5: Credit card company refuses (they send a letter saying “we don’t accept this payment”).
Step 6: Under §3-603(b), their refusal results in DISCHARGE of your obligation to the extent of $1,000.
Step 7: The debt is gone. If they try to collect, you assert discharge as an affirmative defense.
💣 “They will refuse. That’s the point. Their refusal triggers your discharge. They are legally required to refuse because if they accepted, they’d have to credit your account. So they play into your trap every time.”
