Step One: Understand What You Are Doing

You are being sued by a junk debt buyer. They bought your old credit card debt for pennies on the dollar. They are counting on you to do nothing. They are counting on a default judgment.

But the original credit card agreement had a clause. It said disputes can go to private arbitration. That clause was written to protect the creditor. You are going to use it against them.

Why This Works (The Economics)

Debt buyers operate on volume. Court filing fees are low. Most people don't show up. The system is built for speed [citation:1].

Arbitration breaks the system.

When you compel arbitration, the debt buyer has to pay:

  • AAA: $375 filing fee (business side), $1,400 case management, $1,500 arbitrator compensation [citation:2][citation:8]
  • JAMS: $2,500 filing fee, 13% case management fee on professional fees [citation:3]

For a $2,000 debt, the math stops working. Many debt buyers dismiss rather than pay [citation:13].

Honest boundary: Arbitration is a forum change, not an automatic win. You still have to prepare your case. The debt buyer may still pursue. But the economics shift in your favor [citation:1].
What You Need Before You Start
  • Your original credit card agreement (or the one the debt buyer attached to the complaint)
  • The arbitration clause language
  • The case number and court name from the summons
  • The plaintiff's attorney name and address

Step Three: The Cost Comparison

This is why the debt buyer does not want to go to arbitration. Show them the math.

What You Pay (AAA)

$225
Maximum consumer filing fee. Capped by AAA rules [citation:2].

What They Pay (AAA)

$3,275+
$375 filing + $1,400 case management + $1,500 arbitrator compensation [citation:2][citation:8].

What You Pay (JAMS)

$250
Consumer filing fee. All other costs borne by the company [citation:14].

What They Pay (JAMS)

$2,500+
Filing fee plus 13% case management on professional fees [citation:3].
The leverage: If your debt is under $3,000, the debt buyer is losing money by pursuing arbitration. Many dismiss. Some settle for far less. The math is on your side.
When this does NOT work: If the debt is large (e.g., $15,000+), the debt buyer may absorb the arbitration cost. If they have strong documentation, they may pursue. If you are already in trial, it may be too late [citation:1]. This is a strategic tool, not a guaranteed win.

  1. Print three copies of the motion.

  4. Keep the third copy for yourself.
  5. Attach a copy of the credit card agreement with the arbitration clause highlighted.

Step Five: What Happens After You File

The Timeline
Week 1-2: Filing
The clerk stamps your motion. You mail the certified copy to the attorney. The clock starts.
Week 2-4: Response
The plaintiff's attorney may file an objection. This is normal. They will argue you waited too long, or that the clause does not apply. The court decides.
Week 4-8: Ruling
The judge grants or denies the motion. If granted, the court case is stayed (paused) or dismissed. You then initiate arbitration with AAA or JAMS.
After Grant: Arbitration
You file with the arbitration forum. You pay your $225 (AAA) or $250 (JAMS). The debt buyer has to pay their share. Often, they dismiss instead. If they pursue, you present your case to the arbitrator.
If They Object (They Probably Will)

Common objections and your responses:

"You waited too long." — The agreement says arbitration can be elected "as long as a judgment has not been entered" or "as long as trial has not started." Check your agreement. If neither has happened, you are within your rights [citation:4].

"The clause doesn't apply to assignees." — Most credit card agreements explicitly state that the arbitration clause applies to successors and assigns. The debt buyer is an assignee. They are bound [citation:4].

"This is a small claims case." — If there is an attorney involved, it is not small claims court. Debt buyers always use attorneys. Check your local rules [citation:1].

If the motion is denied: You are back in court. You have not lost. You have preserved your defenses. You can still negotiate, settle, or fight. The motion is a tool, not a magic spell.
The final word: You are not helpless. The system was built for volume. You are introducing friction. Friction costs money. Money changes the math. The math changes the outcome.
