📊 The Tension Meter – Zero to Zero

🔥 Brandon’s rule: “I want the whole interaction to start at zero and end at zero on the tension meter. Every word either increases or decreases tension. Your goal: decrease.”
🧠 Why tension spikes: Cops operate under the assumption that humans are violent animals. When you say “na na you can’t get me” or reach around your truck, they think GUNS. Fear spikes → tension spikes → de-escalation becomes nearly impossible.

Captain Morgan’s video (the “first time” example): Hands low, window barely down, tall truck, female officer. “She was terrified — looking for weapons, checking for backup. That’s a recipe for disaster.”

🛡️ Better approach: Hands on steering wheel (visible), window all the way down, calm voice. “I’m a diplomat. I train ambassadors. I’m recording this for educational purposes. I’ll cooperate fully.”

⚠️ The Forbidden Phrase – “You have no jurisdiction”

💣 Statistical fact (from hundreds of arrests): “Every single time someone messages me that they got arrested, I trace it back to the exact moment they said ‘na na you can’t get me’ or some variation. That’s what starts the downfall.”

Why: Cops hear “I don’t recognize your authority” → they assume you’re a sovereign citizen with guns → they escalate to regain control. Instead, reframe:

✅ Low‑tension alternative: “I’m not a resident of any of the 50 states. I’m a non-citizen national. I have a passport. I’m not a driver under title 49. How can I help you verify my identity without committing perjury?”

🔍 “Identify” = Sameness – The Legal Trap They Don’t Know

📖 Definition of “identify”: “to establish sameness between two things.” The officer is trying to prove you are the SAME as the ALL CAPS ens legis (the strawman). That’s a physical impossibility.

Courtroom move (not roadside): “You are attempting to attach a natural person to an artificial person and claim sameness. There is no sameness. I am not failing to identify — you are failing to articulate what sameness you seek.”

Roadside (de‑escalated): “I want to identify, but I’m not sure how without lying under penalty of perjury. Can you help me understand how to identify correctly?” (Flip the problem to them.)

⚖️ Reasonable Articulable Suspicion – The Real Standard

🚨 If you have a registration sticker: Under 34 USC §12611 (Motor Vehicle Theft Prevention Program), police do NOT need RAS to stop you. They can stop to verify you are the registered owner. If you have NO registration sticker, they DO need RAS.

“Most cops don’t know this. They think they always need RAS. If they stop you without RAS and you have no sticker, that’s an illegal stop — grounds for dismissal + counterclaim.”

RAS definition: Specific, articulable facts that lead a reasonable officer to believe criminal activity is afoot. Speeding? Measured by radar/lidar (must be calibrated). No license plate? That’s a equipment violation — still requires reasonable suspicion that a crime occurred (not an assumption).

🏷️ “Registered Owner” = Security Interest (Pink Slip Trust)

📜 Registered owner (Black’s Law): “The owner whose name is recorded in a security issuer’s registers as the securities owner, entitled to receive dividends or interest.”

When you hold a certificate of title (pink slip), you are the REGISTERED OWNER — which means you have a SECURITY INTEREST in the vehicle. The state is the trustee. You are the beneficiary. That’s why police can stop you — they are private security for the trustee.

💡 Solution: Destroy the pink slip. Send affidavit to DMV: “All security interests released. This vehicle is no longer in commerce. I am not a registered owner. Destroy all records.” Combine with private DOT number and diplomatic plates to exit the system entirely.

⚔️ Counterclaim – The Real Win (Not Dismissal)

🎯 Don’t just get the ticket dismissed. Counterclaim. Answer each line of the ticket “DENIED” (puts burden of proof on them), then add your causes of action. Now you’re the plaintiff.

Possible counterclaim causes of action:
- Violation of 15 USC §1 (restraint of trade/commerce) – up to $100M / 10 years prison.
- Violation of 18 USC §1545 (interfering with passport holder) – 10 years prison.
- Involuntary servitude (13th Amendment) – forcing you into a contract without consideration.
- Fraud (definition: intentional perversion of truth to induce surrender of legal right).

📌 Procedure: File “Answer and Counterclaim” in the same document. Numerically continue from the ticket’s line items. If they file a motion to dismiss, you now have leverage to move into Discovery (depositions, interrogatories, subpoenas). “Once you get Discovery, you own them.”

🎥 Discovery & Depositions – “Better Than Sex”

🧨 Deposition: In‑person questioning under penalty of perjury. Video recorded. Ask the officer: “Do you know the definition of ‘driver’ under title 49?” “Do you know the difference between detain and arrest?” (They usually don’t.)

“If I had to choose between sex and a deposition, I would choose a deposition. You can destroy a 20‑year veteran’s entire career in 30 minutes on video.”

Interrogatories: Written questions under oath. Subpoenas: demand documents, third‑party records. Once you successfully counterclaim and move into Discovery, the entire judicial system’s power is at your fingertips.

📋 Assumption Warfare – Challenge Everything

“They assume: you are a resident, your name identifies you, the vehicle is a motor vehicle, you are a driver, registration is mandatory, the CVC applies to you, they have a duty, you consented, etc. There are 18+ assumptions. Challenge each one. Once you challenge an assumption, they must prove it. Most can’t.”

Example: “Officer, you assume I’m a resident of California. I’m not. I’m a non‑resident alien. Please prove I’m a resident under 26 USC §7701.” They can’t — because you’re not.

🚙 Traveling vs. Driving – Brandon’s New Take (Don’t Say It)

⚠️ Brandon’s 2024 advice: “I would never bring up ‘traveling vs driving’ on the side of the road. It’s so associated with sovereign citizens that it spikes tension instantly. Instead, use the definition of ‘driver’ from title 49 and say ‘I’m not a driver under federal law.’ Same legal effect, zero trigger words.”
📄 49 CFR §390.5 – Driver definition: “Driver means any person who operates any commercial motor vehicle.”
Person definition (same section): “individual, partnership, association, corporation, business trust...”
Commercial motor vehicle: used in interstate commerce, 10,001+ lbs.
→ “I’m not operating a commercial motor vehicle in interstate commerce. Thus I’m not a driver.”
