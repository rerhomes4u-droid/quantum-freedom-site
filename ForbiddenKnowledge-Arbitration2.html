Step One: Understand What You Are Doing

You are being sued by a junk debt buyer. They bought your old credit card debt for pennies on the dollar. They are counting on you to do nothing. They are counting on a default judgment.

But the original credit card agreement had a clause. It said disputes can go to private arbitration. That clause was written to protect the creditor. You are going to use it against them.

Why This Works (The Economics)

Debt buyers operate on volume. Court filing fees are low. Most people don't show up. The system is built for speed.

Arbitration breaks the system.

When you compel arbitration, the debt buyer has to pay:

  • AAA: $375 filing fee (business side), $1,400 case management, plus arbitrator compensation at $300/hour [citation:22]
  • JAMS: $2,500 filing fee, plus 13% case management fee on professional fees [citation:4]

For a $2,000 debt, the math stops working. Many debt buyers dismiss rather than pay.

Honest boundary: Arbitration is a forum change, not an automatic win. You still have to prepare your case. The debt buyer may still pursue. But the economics shift in your favor [citation:5].
What You Need Before You Start
  • Your original credit card agreement (or the one the debt buyer attached to the complaint)
  • The arbitration clause language
  • The case number and court name from the summons
  • The plaintiff's attorney name and address

What You Pay (JAMS)

$250
Consumer filing fee. All other costs borne by the company [citation:15].

What They Pay (JAMS)

$2,500+

The leverage: If your debt is under $3,000, the debt buyer is losing money by pursuing arbitration. Many dismiss. Some settle for far less. The math is on your side.
When this does NOT work: If the debt is large (e.g., $15,000+), the debt buyer may absorb the arbitration cost. If they have strong documentation, they may pursue. If you are already in trial, it may be too late [citation:5]. This is a strategic tool, not a guaranteed win.

Step Five: What Happens After You File

The Timeline
Week 1-2: Filing
The clerk stamps your motion. You mail the certified copy to the attorney. The clock starts.
Week 2-4: Response
The plaintiff's attorney may file an objection. This is normal. They will argue you waited too long, or that the clause does not apply. The court decides.
Week 4-8: Ruling
The judge grants or denies the motion. If granted, the court case is stayed (paused) or dismissed. You then initiate arbitration with AAA or JAMS.
Common Objections and Your Responses
"You waited too long."
The agreement says arbitration can be elected "as long as a judgment has not been entered" or "as long as trial has not started." Check your agreement. If neither has happened, you are within your rights. Most courts enforce the clause as written [citation:5].
"The clause doesn't apply to assignees."
Most credit card agreements explicitly state that the arbitration clause applies to successors and assigns. Courts have held that debt buyers who purchase accounts step into the shoes of the original creditor and are bound by the arbitration clause [citation:14][citation:20]. The debt buyer is an assignee. They are bound.
"This is a small claims case."
If there is an attorney involved, it is not small claims court. Debt buyers always use attorneys. Check your local rules. If the case was filed in a court where attorneys are not allowed, the arbitration clause may not apply [citation:5].
"The clause doesn't cover this dispute."
Most arbitration clauses are broad: "all disputes arising from or relating to the account." A debt collection lawsuit arises from the account. The clause covers it.
After the Motion is Granted: Starting Arbitration
Step 1: Choose Your Forum
If the agreement names AAA or JAMS, use that forum. If it says "either," choose the one that is more convenient for you.
Step 2: File Your Demand
Go to the forum's website. Download the consumer demand form. Fill it out. Attach a copy of the arbitration clause and the court order granting your motion. Pay your filing fee ($225 for AAA, $250 for JAMS).
Step 3: Serve the Debt Buyer
The forum will send a copy to the debt buyer. They now have to pay their share. This is the moment the math hits them.
Step 4: Wait
Many debt buyers dismiss at this point. The cost of arbitration exceeds the value of the debt. If they pursue, you prepare your case. You present your evidence to the arbitrator. The same rules of evidence apply, but the process is faster and more informal than court.
If They Want to Settle (They Probably Will)

Once you file the motion, the debt buyer's attorney may reach out to settle. Here is a script:

The Settlement Script
"I am not interested in a payment plan. I am interested in resolving this matter. I have filed a motion to compel arbitration. If the court grants it, you will have to pay thousands of dollars in arbitration fees for a debt you bought for pennies. I am willing to settle for [amount, e.g., 10-20% of the alleged debt] as a full and final resolution. If you agree, I will dismiss the motion and we can be done. If not, I will see you in arbitration."
Honest boundary: Only make an offer you can afford. Get any settlement in writing before you pay anything. Do not pay until you have a signed agreement that says the matter is resolved and no further collection will occur.

You are not helpless.
