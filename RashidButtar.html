A Controversial Figure

Who Dr. Rashid Buttar was

Dr. Rashid Ali Buttar was an osteopathic physician, author, and prominent figure in the "medical freedom" movement. He gained international attention during the COVID-19 pandemic for his outspoken opposition to vaccines and his claims about depopulation plans. He was named one of the "Disinformation Dozen" by the Center for Countering Digital Hate in 2021 — a designation he wore as a badge of honor.

Born in 1966, Buttar practiced medicine in Cornelius, North Carolina, at the Centers for Advanced Medicine. He was licensed by the North Carolina Medical Board in 1995. Before his death, he claimed to have been poisoned after a CNN interview in 2021, and later suffered a stroke and myocarditis in February 2023. He died on May 18, 2023, at the age of 57.

⚠️ The Circumstances

Dr. Buttar died on May 18, 2023, at his home in North Carolina. His family and the Centers for Advanced Medicine announced his death but did not disclose a cause. His death came days after he claimed in an interview that he had been "poisoned with 200 times the amount of what was in the vaccinations." However, according to multiple sources, Buttar had been seriously ill since at least 2016 with diagnosed coronary artery disease — years before COVID-19 existed.

His Advocacy

What Dr. Buttar spoke out about

💉 COVID-19 Vaccine Opposition

Buttar was one of the most prominent voices opposing COVID-19 vaccines. He claimed the vaccines would "modify your DNA" and that "most people who took the vaccine will be dead by 2025." He told CNN: "More people are dying from the vaccine than from COVID-19."[citation:8]

Vaccine Skepticism

🌍 Depopulation Claims

Buttar frequently claimed that COVID-19 was part of a planned depopulation agenda. He called it a "mass genocide" and claimed the pandemic was "planned" and "politically motivated." He wrote a book titled "Poisoned: A Deep Dive Into The Mass Bioweapon & Envenomation Agenda."[citation:4][citation:17]

Conspiracy Theory

🦠 "Best Thing is to Get COVID"

In his CNN interview, Buttar said: "I've told people the best thing that could happen is you get COVID-19. You're going to build your own innate immune system, and then you don't have to worry about it anymore." He also likened Dr. Anthony Fauci to Adolf Hitler.[citation:8][citation:17]

Controversial Claim

🏆 Disinformation Dozen

In 2021, the Center for Countering Digital Hate named Buttar one of the "Disinformation Dozen" — twelve individuals responsible for producing approximately 65% of all anti-vaccine content on Facebook and Twitter. Buttar and others treated the designation as a badge of honor.[citation:11]

Designation

💬 "Never Take the Vaccine"

Buttar stated: "I've said publicly that you would have to shoot me in the head with lead – i.e. a bullet – because I'm never going to take the vaccine." He maintained this position until his death.[citation:17]

Personal Conviction

📚 Author & Speaker

Buttar authored multiple books and was a sought-after speaker in the "health freedom" community. His book "Poisoned" detailed his claims about bioweapons and depopulation agendas.[citation:4]

The Controversy

Medical board charges and patient exploitation

🚨 Medical Board Disciplinary Actions

Dr. Buttar was disciplined by the North Carolina Medical Board multiple times. In 2007, the Board charged him with unprofessional conduct for his treatment of cancer patients. In 2010, he was disciplined again. In 2019, he faced further disciplinary action. The Board also found that he exploited patients by charging exorbitant fees for ineffective treatments.[citation:3][citation:4]

💰 Exploiting Cancer Patients

The North Carolina Medical Board found that Buttar charged cancer patients tens of thousands of dollars for unproven and ineffective treatments. Patient A paid over $27,000 for intravenous hydrogen peroxide. Patient B paid over $30,000. Patient C paid over $32,000. All patients eventually died of their cancers.[citation:5][citation:10]

Board Finding

📋 "100% Success Rate" Claim

Buttar told cancer patients he had a "100% success rate." A widow of one patient said: "He said it didn't matter what kind of cancer anybody had, he could cure it. He kept reiterating he had a 100 percent success rate." The Board found these representations were false and made with full knowledge of their falsity.[citation:5][citation:19]

False Claims

🏥 Nurse Practitioner Doing the Work

The Board found that Buttar's patients were "seen and treated mainly by Dr. Buttar's nurse practitioner." Despite having little or no personal interaction with patients, Buttar charged thousands of dollars for his "services." All clinical notes for Patient B were written by his nurse practitioner.[citation:5]

Lack of Supervision

💸 Collection from Estates

After one patient died, Buttar referred the account to a collection agency and sought over $25,000 from the widow. Another patient's widow cancelled a check for $6,700 because she felt the treatments were useless. Buttar pursued collection from her as well.[citation:5]

Patient Exploitation

🧪 Unproven Therapies

Buttar's treatments included intravenous hydrogen peroxide, EDTA chelation, chromium, and various vitamins — none of which have known value for treating cancer. The Board found these therapies were "unproven and wholly ineffective."[citation:10]

Ineffective Treatments

📉 Health Issues Since 2016

According to multiple sources, Buttar had been seriously ill since at least 2016 with diagnosed coronary artery disease. This was seven years before his death and years before COVID-19 existed. His own book indicated he had been ill since 2016.[citation:4]
