02The Search Protocol: How to Find What You Are Looking For

The Method

The archive is only useful if you know how to search it. Here is the protocol. Name the specific. Find the corroboration. Mark the gap. Refuse to overclaim.

The Protocol

Search by name. Start with the people who are already in the public record. Search for the name. Look at the documents linked to that entity. Look at the flights. Look at the dates. Do not assume guilt. Look for corroboration.

Search by document. If you have a document number or a file name, search for it. The archive has full-text search. It will show you the document, the entities mentioned, and the other documents linked to it.

Search by flight. The flight logs are the strongest corroboration. They place people in specific places at specific times. Search the flight logs. Cross-reference the dates with the documents.

Search by media. The media archive has 500+ verified files. Search for the people you are investigating. Look at the photos. Look at the dates.

Trace to the source. Every document in the archive is linked to its original DOJ filing. Click the source link. Verify the document yourself. Do not take the archive's word for it.

The Warning

A name in the files is not a conviction. The DOJ list includes people who were "simply referenced in the documents, or in news articles included in the files." A name is a starting point, not a verdict.

The redactions are the gap. Six men identified as "possibly involved in wrongdoing" remain redacted. The archive cannot show you what the DOJ has not released.

The archive is not complete. The DOJ has removed and retroactively redacted documents. Independent researchers are building parallel archives because the official release is incomplete [citation:14].

GAP: The archive is a tool. It is not the truth. It is a map. The map is not the territory. The reader must verify every claim against the original source.
