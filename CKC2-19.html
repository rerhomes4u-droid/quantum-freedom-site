🚫 Title 18 = Criminal Only (No Private Right of Action)

“18 USC §242 (deprivation of rights under color of law) is a CRIMINAL statute. You cannot sue for civil damages under Title 18. The court will dismiss. I learned this the hard way in my American Express case.”

The fix: Use the CIVIL equivalent: 42 USC §1983 (same language, but private right of action). For conspiracy: 42 USC §1985.

💣 “I rewrote all my cases after my first dismissal. Now I use the civil codes. Don’t make my mistake.”

⚔️ 42 USC §1983 – Civil Action for Deprivation of Rights

“Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage, subjects any citizen of the United States or other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured in an action at law, suit in equity, or other proper proceeding for redress.”

Use this to sue: police officers, government officials, judges (with caution), and any person acting “under color of law.” This is the primary civil rights enforcement statute. No qualified immunity if they acted unreasonably (break down “reasonably” with definitions).

🎯 “If a cop pulls you over and violates your rights, you don’t need a lawyer. You can file in forma pauperis (IFP) – no filing fees. Sue them individually. Take their badge. Take their money.”

👥 42 USC §1985 – Conspiracy (Two or More Persons)

“If two or more persons conspire to deprive any person of the equal protection of the laws, or of equal privileges and immunities under the laws, the party so injured may have an action for the recovery of damages.”

Bigger teeth than §1983. When an officer calls for backup and they work together to violate your rights, that’s a conspiracy under §1985. Damages are trebled in some cases. Use this as your “nuclear” civil rights claim.

💀 “1983 is a 2‑inch fan with venom. 1985 is a bazooka. If two or more officers are involved, USE 1985. Sue them together AND individually under 1983. File in forma pauperis – zero cost to you.”

🛡️ Qualified Immunity – The Cop’s Shield (And How to Break It)

Qualified immunity protects officials from suit unless they violated “clearly established” rights that a reasonable official would have known. Key word: REASONABLE.

Attack strategy: Break down the word “reasonable” – ordinary, rational, appropriate. Show that the officer’s actions were NOT reasonable because you clearly explained (on body cam) that you were not a “driver” under 49 CFR §390.5, that your vehicle was not a “commercial motor vehicle,” that you are a non‑citizen national with a passport, etc. Qualified immunity does NOT apply to suits against the government entity itself (police department, city). Sue the entity AND the officer.

🔥 “If they had body cam footage of you calmly explaining the law and they still violated your rights, they are NOT acting reasonably. Qualified immunity fails. You win.”

💰 In Forma Pauperis – Sue Without a Penny

IFP (“in the manner of a pauper”) allows you to file a civil lawsuit without prepaying filing fees. The court defers fees until after you win, then fees are taken from the judgment.

“Living in your car? On a park bench? No problem. File IFP. You don’t need a single Federal Reserve note to sue police officers for violating your rights. The court will approve IFP for nearly everyone.”

📜 Attorney‑in‑Fact vs. Attorney‑at‑Law (You Don’t Need a License)

Attorney‑at‑law = bar card member, “officer of the court.” When you hire one, you become a “client” – a ward of the court, essentially an infant or person of unsound mind.
Attorney‑in‑fact = non‑bar agent acting under a power of attorney. No license required. You retain all your rights. The attorney‑in‑fact stands as you, not representing you.

“I am an attorney‑in‑fact. I am not licensed by any bar. I act under a power of attorney between me and the ALL CAPS name. This is completely legal and more powerful than being a ‘client.’ Read the Power of Attorney Act in your state.”

✍️ “A ‘license’ means permission to do something that would otherwise be not allowable. Bar cards are membership cards, not licenses. Ask an attorney‑at‑law for his ‘license’ – he will show you a bar card. That’s not a license. It’s a private club membership.”

“Every refusal, every ‘go fuck yourself’ letter, every stamped ‘received’ – that’s EVIDENCE. Don’t be upset when they deny you. Be happy. You just got a piece of paper you can use in court.”
