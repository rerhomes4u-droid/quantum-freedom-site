01 The Collapse: $40 Million Missing, a Republican Insider

The Claim

The Franklin Community Federal Credit Union was a front. The money missing from it funded a nationwide child trafficking network for the elite.

Record: Credit union collapsed, King incompetent to stand trial Suppressed: The money funded trafficking
The Record

The Franklin Community Federal Credit Union collapsed in November 1988. $39–40 million was missing [citation:3][citation:11].

Lawrence E. "Larry" King Jr. was the manager. He was a Republican Party activist who sang the national anthem at the 1984 Republican National Convention [citation:3].

A federal magistrate ruled King mentally incompetent to stand trial in March 1990. The diagnosis was "probable delusional paranoid disorder — grandiose type." The magistrate recommended commitment for psychiatric treatment [citation:3].

King never stood trial for the financial charges. The trafficking allegations were investigated separately.

The Suppressed

The trafficking allegations were real. A Nebraska legislative committee investigated. The allegations expanded from financial fraud to a nationwide child abuse ring involving "businessmen, media personalities, lawmen and educators" [citation:10].

The committee's chief investigator died. "Suddenly, and violently, like more than a dozen other people linked to the Franklin case" [citation:11].

The money was never accounted for. The credit union's collapse was the entry point. The trafficking was the story underneath.

GAP: The credit union collapse and the missing money are documented. King's incompetence is documented. The trafficking allegations were investigated and rejected by grand juries. The chief investigator died. The record does not connect the money to the trafficking. It does not disconnect them either.

02 The Grand Jury: "A Carefully Crafted Hoax"

The Claim

Multiple grand juries found the trafficking allegations were unfounded. The "Franklin scandal" was a hoax.

Record: County and federal grand juries rejected the allegations Suppressed: The grand jury was part of the cover-up
The Record

A county grand jury concluded the "lurid reports of child sex abuse, drug trafficking, pornography, and political intrigue" were a "carefully crafted hoax." The grand jury indicted two people for perjured testimony [citation:10].

A federal grand jury agreed the allegations were unfounded [citation:10].

The New York Times reported the allegations were "based on still unverified reports from half a dozen young people" [citation:10].

PolitiFact rates the claim that the scandal was real as "False." The fact-check cites the grand jury findings [citation:10].

The Suppressed

The grand jury's conclusion rested on the credibility of Paul Bonacci. Bonacci was a key witness with multiple personality disorder. The grand jury said he was "not capable of telling the truth" [citation:1].

The psychiatrist who examined Bonacci for the committee disagreed. Dr. Densen-Gerber testified: "It would be very difficult for Mr. Bonacci to lie... He does not fabricate" [citation:1].

The police-hired psychiatrist agreed. Dr. Beverly Mead: "I personally feel that these details that he's related to us were experiences that he really experienced" [citation:1].

Bonacci reported the abuse in 1986—two years before the Franklin case broke—to Omaha police and school officials. The school counselor confirmed it [citation:1].

GAP: The grand jury said hoax. The psychiatrists said the witness was credible. The witness reported the abuse two years before the case broke. The record contains both. It does not resolve the contradiction.

03 The Finders: The CIA-Adjacent Case

The Claim

A cult called the Finders was investigated for child abuse. The CIA had connections to the group. The investigation was closed.

Record: Charges dropped, CIA denied involvement Suppressed: DOJ investigated CIA cover-up
The Record

In February 1987, two men from a commune called the Finders were found in a Florida park with six children. The children were dirty and bug-bitten. Charges of child neglect were dropped for lack of evidence [citation:5].

The CIA denied any involvement. A spokesman said: "Any claim that we obstructed justice in this case is nuts" [citation:4].

Search warrants in Virginia "did not produce evidence of Federal or State violations of the law concerning sexual exploitation of children" [citation:13].

The Suppressed

A Justice Department task force investigated whether the CIA used the Finders as a front and whether the CIA tried to shut down the child abuse investigation [citation:4].

The Finders' leader's wife had been a CIA employee. One of his sons had worked for Air America, a CIA cutout used to traffic heroin during the Vietnam War [citation:12].

A computer-training company that employed Finders members trained CIA personnel. The company's president denied it was owned by the Finders [citation:4].

The FBI files were released in 2019, heavily redacted. A list of names and addresses was found on the Finders men caught in Florida [citation:12].

  • Archive.org: "Eye of the Chickenhawk" (2023) — Finders FBI files analysis archive.org The 2019 FBI file release. The redactions. The names list. The CIA connections.
GAP: The charges were dropped. The CIA denied involvement. The DOJ investigated whether the CIA covered it up. The files are redacted. The record does not prove a CIA child trafficking ring. It shows an investigation that was closed, a denial, and a redacted file.
