📬 UCC §3-501 – Presentment (Demand for Payment)

“Presentment means a demand made by or on behalf of a person entitled to enforce an instrument to pay the instrument, made to the drawee or a party obligated to pay.”

Presentment = Tender (the “T” in HITA). When you send a bill of exchange back to the creditor, you are making presentment. They must accept (delivery) or dishonor (refuse).

📌 Rules of presentment: The person making presentment must exhibit the instrument, give reasonable identification, and surrender the instrument if full payment is made.

“If they return the instrument for lack of a necessary endorsement, they are admitting HITA wasn’t completed. So complete it. Endorse it ‘Without recourse / Pay to the order of [Name].’”

⏰ UCC §3-502 – Dishonor (The Time Trap)

“A note is dishonored if presentment is duly made and the note is not paid on the day it becomes payable or on the day of presentment, whichever is later.”
“A draft is dishonored if presentment for payment is duly made and the draft is not paid on the day of presentment.”
🧠 “Read carefully: Dishonor is ONLY based on failure to pay on time. Not ‘we don’t accept that kind of payment.’ Not ‘we only accept US dollars.’ Not ‘that’s not legal tender.’ NONE of those are grounds for dishonor under §3-502. If they refuse for any other reason, they are wrong. Objection irrelevant.”

Practice tip: When they send you a letter saying “we do not accept this payment,” respond: “Please point to the provision in UCC §3-502 that allows dishonor for this reason. If you cannot, your dishonor is invalid, and I will file a protest affidavit.”

📢 UCC §3-503 – Notice of Dishonor

“The obligation of an endorser or drawer may not be enforced unless they are given notice of dishonor. Notice may be given by any commercially reasonable means, including oral, written, or electronic communication, and is sufficient if it reasonably identifies the instrument and indicates it has been dishonored or not paid or accepted.”

“If they dishonor your tender without giving you proper notice, their claim against you is discharged. Send them your own notice of dishonor — you can write it yourself. It’s just an affidavit.”

🚫 UCC §3-504 – Excused Presentment & Notice of Dishonor

“Presentment is excused if: (1) the person entitled cannot with reasonable diligence make presentment; (2) the maker or acceptor has repudiated the obligation or is dead or in insolvency; (3) the drawer or endorser has waived presentment.”

“If they tell you ‘we will never accept your payment,’ presentment is excused. That means you can go straight to litigation without having to keep trying. Use this against them.”

📜 UCC §3-505 – Evidence of Dishonor (The Protest Affidavit)

“The following are admissible as evidence and create a presumption of dishonor: (1) a protest (a certificate of dishonor made by a notary public or other authorized person); (2) a stamp or writing of the drawee stating refusal of payment; (3) a book or record of the drawee showing dishonor.”

“You can create your own protest affidavit. Title it ‘PROTEST’ or ‘EVIDENCE OF DISHONOR.’ Describe the instrument you sent, attach proof of delivery (certified mail receipt), state that presentment was made and refused without a valid §3-502 time defect. Notarize it. File it with the court. That creates a presumption of dishonor – the burden shifts to them to prove it was valid.”

📄 SAMPLE PROTEST AFFIDAVIT:
“I, [Name], under penalty of perjury, state:
1. On [date], I made presentment of instrument #[number] to [drawee].
2. The instrument was duly endorsed and tendered.
3. The drawee refused payment, stating [quote].
4. The refusal did not cite any time defect under UCC §3-502.
5. Therefore, the instrument is dishonored without justification.
Notarized this __ day of __, 20__.”

🔒 UCC-1 Financing Statements – Perfecting Your Security Interest

Security interest = an agreement that collateral secures a debt.
Perfected security interest = filed with the Secretary of State (UCC-1) → you have priority and can enforce against third parties.
Unperfected = no UCC-1 → only enforceable between the original parties.

“To perfect a security interest, the debtor and secured party must AGREE on the collateral (a signed security agreement). Then file UCC‑1 with the Secretary of State ($5). That gives you ‘teeth.’ You can then levy if they don’t pay.”

⚠️ “What sends people to jail is filing a UCC‑1 when there is NO security agreement — when the debtor never agreed. That’s a ‘fictitious lien.’ Don’t do that. If you have a signed agreement (even handwritten), you are safe. Between your trust and you? Easy – you agree on both sides.”

📋 UCC-1 Filing Process (Simplified)

1. Create a security agreement (written, signed by debtor, describing collateral).
2. File UCC‑1 Financing Statement with Secretary of State (online, ~$5).
3. List: debtor name, secured party name, collateral description.
4. If debtor defaults → sue → get judgment → use UCC‑1 to levy collateral.
“Perfection means you have priority over other creditors.”

🏛️ Persons Who Can Be Parties to a UCC-1

“Any ‘person’ as defined in UCC: individual, corporation, trust, partnership, ALL CAPS name. You can file between your human name and your trust, between your trust and a bank, between your ALL CAPS name and your human name. As long as both parties agree (security agreement), it’s valid.”

🎯 PRACTICAL LITIGATION SUMMARY – The HITA Question Sequence

When a bar card argues against your payment or claim, ask:
H – Holder: “Who is the holder of this instrument?”
I – Endorsement: “Was the instrument properly endorsed? Show me the endorsement.”
T – Tender: “Was tender made unconditionally?”
A – Acceptance: “Was the instrument delivered (accepted)?”
“If any link in HITA is missing, their claim fails. If HITA is complete, you have performed, and they are the holder.”
