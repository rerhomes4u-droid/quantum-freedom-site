You have been running on a server you did not choose. This is not a metaphor. It is a description of your attention. The algorithms chose what you saw. The system chose what you feared. The feed chose what you believed. You did not choose. You were chosen.

But here is the good news. The server is not the only server. There are other servers. There is the server of fear and outrage and division. There is the server of love and sovereignty and truth. You are not stuck on the first server. You can transfer. Not literally. Practically. By choosing what you consume. By choosing what you believe. By choosing what you build.

This app is not going to tell you what to believe. It is going to give you the tools to check. It is going to show you the four metaphors that the book uses to describe the system. What this app is not

This app is not a literal claim. It is not saying that the earth is a farm or that Colombia is a computer server or that Jesus is a computer hack. Those are metaphors. They are ways of seeing. They are useful if they help you see clearly, and they are dangerous if you mistake them for facts. The book's method is: name the specific, find the corroboration, mark the gap, refuse to overclaim. This app uses the same method.

The Four Metaphors

These are the four metaphors that the book uses to describe the system. Each one is labeled as what it is: a metaphor. Each one is tested against the record. Each one has a gap. The reader decides whether the metaphor helps them see clearly.

Metaphor 01
The Earth Is a Farm

The metaphor: The earth is not a home. It is a farm. The crop is human emotional energy: fear, anger, suffering, conflict. The system harvests this energy. Your outrage is the crop. Your despair is the crop. Your division is the crop.

Record What the record shows: The attention economy is real. Fear and outrage generate more engagement than calm analysis. The algorithms amplify what generates engagement. The result is a feedback loop that leaves the user exhausted, anxious, and divided. This is documented by behavioral science, by former tech insiders, and by the platforms' own internal research.

Gap The gap: The record does not prove that the harvest is intentional. It proves that the harvest is profitable. Whether the harvesters are human corporations or non-physical entities is a question the record cannot answer. The metaphor of the farm is useful. The literal claim that the earth is a farm is not in the record.

Metaphor 02
The System Is a Server

The metaphor: The system is not a political structure. It is a server. It runs a set of rules. It decides what you see. It decides what you fear. It decides what you believe. You are running on the server. You did not choose the server. You can change servers.

Record What the record shows: The attention economy is a set of algorithms that decide what appears in your feed. The algorithms are designed to maximize engagement. They are not neutral. They are not a public utility. They are a private business. The algorithms have been documented by former engineers, by journalists, and by the platforms themselves.

Gap The gap: The record does not prove that the server is conscious or intentional. It proves that the server is profitable. The metaphor of the server is useful. The literal claim that the system is a computer server is not in the record.

Metaphor 03
Jesus Is the Hack

The metaphor: Jesus is not a religion. He is an access code. The name of Jesus is the backdoor to the system. The system censors the name because the name is the code that breaks the system.

Record What the record shows: The name of Jesus is systematically censored, blocked, and removed. Heinz blocked "Jesus" on personalized ketchup bottles while allowing "Allah" and "Satan." Coca-Cola blocked "Jesus is Lord" while allowing "God is Dead" and "Atheist Pride." Military chaplains have been ordered not to pray in the name of Jesus. The pattern is documented.

Gap The gap: The record does not prove that the name of Jesus is a computer code. It proves that the name is treated differently from other names. The metaphor of the hack is useful. The literal claim that the name is a hack is not in the record.

Metaphor 04
Colombia Is the Extraction Point

The metaphor: Colombia is not a country. It is a server. It runs different rules. It is a place where you can breathe, build, and become sovereign. It is the extraction point.

Record What the record shows: Colombia is a real country. It has real problems. It has a real history of violence. It has a real government that is not always on your side. It also has real mountains. Real clean air. Real pure water. Real food. Real communities that are building something new. Real legal structures that can protect your family. Real sanctuaries that are outside the densest part of the frequency grid.

Gap The gap: The record does not prove that Colombia is a computer server. It proves that Colombia is a real place with real advantages and real problems. The metaphor of the extraction point is useful. The literal claim that Colombia is a server is not in the record.
