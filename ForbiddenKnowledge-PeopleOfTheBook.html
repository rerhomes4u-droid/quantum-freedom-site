01 The Question: Did Muhammad Exist?

The Claim

There is no sound historical evidence that the prophet of Islam actually existed. The story is fable or fiction, written centuries later.

Record: A live academic debate, not a consensus Suppressed: The question is forbidden
The Record

The skeptical case is real. The Cambridge volume on the earliest writings on Muhammad states the range: assessments go from "largely genuine and credible" to the position of Nevo and Koren that "Muhammad is not a historical figure, and his official biography is a product of the age in which it was written" [citation:1].

The source material is thin. "Practically no manuscripts or papyri from the first century of Islam." Coins with Muhammad's name appear only toward the end of the first century. The literary sources were written 150 to 250 years after the events [citation:1].

Robert Spencer's Did Muhammad Exist? is the most widely circulated popular version of the skeptical case [citation:6][citation:11].

The Suppressed

The suppressed stream says the question is forbidden. You are not allowed to ask it. You are not allowed to look at the evidence. The question itself is the heresy.

The suppressed claim: If the question is forbidden, it is because the answer threatens the system. The system does not want you to look. That is the reason to look.

GAP: The question is academically legitimate. It is not the consensus. The record contains the debate. It does not contain a verdict.

02 The Rebuttal: The Scholars Who Affirm the Existence

The Claim

Even the most skeptical scholars of early Islam affirm that Muhammad existed. The question is not whether he lived, but what we can know about him.

Record: Crone and Cook affirm the existence Gap: The debate is about the details, not the person
The Record

Patricia Crone and Michael Cook are the scholars most associated with skeptical readings of early Islam. Both affirmed Muhammad's historical existence. Crone wrote that a Greek text from 632–634 mentioning a "false prophet" among the Saracens provides "indisputable evidence" that he was a historical figure [citation:12].

Cook wrote that the evidence "eliminates all doubt" about Muhammad as a real person [citation:12].

The academic consensus is that Muhammad existed. The debate is about the reliability of the sources, not the existence of the man.

The Suppressed

The suppressed stream does not want the rebuttal. It wants the question to remain open because the question is the weapon. If Muhammad did not exist, Islam is a fabrication. That is the argument the suppressed stream wants to make.

The suppressed claim: The rebuttal is part of the cover-up. The scholars who affirm the existence are protecting the institution.

GAP: The skeptical case exists. The rebuttal exists. The consensus is that Muhammad existed. The gap is not "did he exist." The gap is: what do you do with the question?
