The Doctor Who Stopped Thalidomide

Who Dr. Claus Knapp was

Dr. Claus Knapp was a Spanish pediatric radiologist who, along with Dr. Widukind Lenz, discovered the link between thalidomide and severe congenital malformations in newborns. Their investigation, conducted by going door-to-door interviewing families after work hours, exposed one of the biggest pharmaceutical scandals of the 20th century and prevented thousands of additional cases worldwide.[citation:7][citation:14]

Born in Madrid in 1928 to German parents, Knapp studied medicine in Spain and completed his postgraduate training in Hamburg. He worked at the University Children's Clinic in Hamburg-Eppendorf, where he noticed a pattern in the X-rays of newborns with mysterious malformations. His determination to find the cause led to the identification of thalidomide as the culprit, and the drug was withdrawn from the German market on November 27, 1961.[citation:1][citation:8]

⚠️ The Circumstances

Dr. Knapp died on February 27, 2026, at the age of 97, in Madrid, Spain. He spent his final decades fighting for justice for thalidomide victims in Spain, where the drug remained on the market until 1985 — 24 years after it was withdrawn in Germany. He prepared nearly 700 expert reports for victims without charging fees and donated his entire archive to AVITE, the Spanish Thalidomide Victims Association.[citation:8][citation:13]

His Life's Mission

The detective work that saved thousands of children

🕵️ The Door-to-Door Investigation

Beginning on October 27, 1961 — Knapp's birthday — he and Dr. Lenz began visiting families after their hospital shifts. They drove through Hamburg in Knapp's used Peugeot, knocking on doors, asking mothers about their pregnancies, their diets, their cosmetics, and the medications they had taken. Within just 17 days, they had identified the cause.[citation:14]

Detective Work

📝 The Landmark 1962 Paper

On July 15, 1962, Lenz and Knapp published their findings in the Deutsche Medizinische Wochenschrift. Their study revealed that 0.17% of babies born in Hamburg in 1961 suffered from "thalidomide embryopathy." They documented that in half of cases only arms were affected, in a quarter both arms and legs, and in a sixth part there were no ears.[citation:8]

Published Evidence

🔬 "Critical Window" Discovery

Knapp and Lenz identified the "critical window" of embryonic exposure: days 36-50 from the last menstrual period. They charted the pattern of malformations and created original classification systems for the cases they studied.[citation:1][citation:15]

Scientific Breakthrough

🌍 Global Impact

The withdrawal of thalidomide in Germany in November 1961, prompted by Lenz and Knapp's findings, led to the drug being banned worldwide. An estimated 10,000 children were affected in Germany alone, but the quick action prevented thousands more. Their work also led to stricter drug testing regulations globally.[citation:7][citation:8]

Global Consequences

🇪🇸 Spain's Shame

Despite the discovery, thalidomide remained on the market in Spain until 1985 — 24 years longer than in Germany. Knapp spent his final years fighting for Spanish victims, who were denied justice when courts ruled the case "prescribed." He wrote to German Chancellor Angela Merkel demanding justice for Spanish victims.[citation:1][citation:15][citation:18]

Unfinished Justice

📋 700 Free Expert Reports

After being rediscovered by AVITE in 2013, Knapp dedicated himself to helping Spanish victims. He prepared nearly 700 expert reports without charging fees, creating a certification system with four categories: "Certain," "Demonstrable," "Probable," and "Not Demonstrable" to validate victims' claims.[citation:8][citation:9]

Lifetime Service

The Attacks on His Integrity

How the pharmaceutical company tried to destroy him

🚨 Grünenthal's War Against the Truth

When Knapp and Lenz's research threatened Grünenthal's profits, the company launched a campaign to destroy their credibility. Knapp later revealed: "Grünenthal wanted to kill Lenz and me." The company hired private detectives to follow them 24 hours a day and pressured them through the press.[citation:8][citation:12]

🕵️ Private Detectives

Grünenthal hired private detectives to follow both Knapp and Lenz around the clock. Knapp recalled: "To Lenz and me, Grünenthal put private detectives to follow us 24 hours a day; but how stupid they were. Grünenthal asked us again and again, who was paying us to say that barbarity, that their medicine was the cause of the malformations."[citation:12]

Surveillance

📰 Press Smear Campaign

The company exerted pressure through the press and rejected their conclusions. Three weeks after their discovery, Grünenthal finally withdrew the drug — but during that time, more children were born with malformations.[citation:8]

Media Manipulation

💬 "Pregnant Woman Who Takes Junk is a Whore"

At a medical congress, Grünenthal representatives said: "The woman who is pregnant and takes junk, that woman is a whore." This shocking statement revealed the company's contempt for victims and their families.[citation:12]

Victim Blaming

🐈 The Falsified Cat Study

Knapp revealed that Grünenthal "was not a serious company, since it made a film with cats and manipulated it, demonstrating that cats that didn't take their medication died. And the cats that took theirs didn't die. But they didn't give anything to their own cats, that's why they didn't die. There was even a trial about that."[citation:12]

Fraudulent Science

🧪 Nazi Concentration Camp Origins

Knapp confirmed the myth that thalidomide was invented in Nazi concentration camps. He stated: "Although I found out later, yes, it's true, in fact, they had working in their factory doctors who had been assigned to those camps." First clinical trials were conducted on Jewish prisoners at Auschwitz.[citation:12]

Dark Origins

🤐 The Cover-Up in Spain

A letter from Grünenthal to its Spanish representative asked them to hide the reality from Spanish doctors so they wouldn't know what caused the malformations. Knapp said: "For me, the factory is totally immoral. The German government played along with them, and they didn't want people talking about Germany manufacturing toxic medicines."[citation:19]

Institutional Cover-Up

"The guilty one, the only way out they have is to deny everything."

— Dr. Claus Knapp, on Grünenthal's tactics

His Legacy

The archive, the foundation, the fight for justice

📁 The Archive Donated to AVITE

In his final wishes, Dr. Knapp specified that his entire documentary archive should be given to AVITE, the Spanish Thalidomide Victims Association. The archive includes original documents from the 1961 withdrawal of thalidomide, research charts used to identify the "critical window," original clinical records and classifications of cases, congress programs, scientific articles, correspondence, and press clippings from various countries.[citation:1][citation:16]

Historical Legacy

📄 The November 27, 1961 Document

Among the most important pieces is an internal communication from the Pharmacy of the Hamburg-Eppendorf University Hospital, dated November 27, 1961, ordering the immediate withdrawal of Contergan (thalidomide) from all hospital services. This document marks the beginning of the end of the thalidomide disaster.[citation:1]

Key Evidence

🏅 Congressional Recognition Proposal

In January 2017, the Ciudadanos parliamentary group presented a Non-Law Proposal to the Spanish Congress requesting the Gran Cruz de Sanidad (Grand Cross of Health) for Dr. Knapp. The proposal stated: "Few Spanish doctors have contributed so decisively to halting a humanitarian catastrophe as that generated by thalidomide."[citation:11]

Official Recognition

✉️ Letter to Angela Merkel

In 2015, Knapp wrote directly to German Chancellor Angela Merkel, demanding justice for Spanish thalidomide victims. He wrote: "It would have been the first time that the company Grünenthal was condemned. And they put all their effort into avoiding it? The fate of the sick seems to matter little to them!"[citation:18]

Advocacy

📊 The Certification System

Knapp developed a certification system to help Spanish victims receive official recognition and compensation. He created four categories — "Certain," "Demonstrable," "Probable," and "Not Demonstrable" — to validate claims based on clinical evidence and probability indicators. His goal was to open the door for Spanish victims to access German pensions of €700-€7,000 monthly.[citation:9]

Practical Help

🕊️ AVITE's Eternal Gratitude

AVITE stated: "Beyond his medical work, Dr. Knapp was a tireless ally and a fundamental human support for all those affected. His commitment to our cause left an indelible mark that we will always honor." Each white rose on the floral tribute sent by AVITE represented a member of the association.[citation:13]

Community Love

— AVITE

⚠️ Critical Note

Dr. Knapp's discovery prevented thousands of additional cases of thalidomide-related malformations worldwide. But in Spain, where the drug remained legal until 1985, victims are still fighting for recognition. As of 2019, only 103 patients had been officially recognized, and no victim had received compensation. Knapp's archive is now a key piece of evidence in that ongoing fight.[citation:15]
