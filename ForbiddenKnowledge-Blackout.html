04 The 15-Minute City: Urban Planning or Containment?

The Claim

The 15-minute city is a containment grid. They want to lock you into a zone and track your movement.

Record: Urban planning concept, peer-reviewed critique exists Suppressed: The design is the cage
The Record

The 15-minute city was popularized by Carlos Moreno in 2016. It aims to reduce car dependency and mitigate global warming by ensuring residents can reach daily necessities within 15 minutes on foot or by bike [citation:4].

A 2025 peer-reviewed critical review notes the concept "has sparked controversy, particularly over its feasibility, economic implications, and social equity concerns" [citation:4].

The concept is not a lockdown plan. It is an urban planning framework. It does not include movement tracking or containment.

The Suppressed

The suppressed claim: The design is the cage. If everything you need is within 15 minutes, you don't need to leave. If you don't need to leave, you can be monitored. If you can be monitored, you can be controlled.

The suppressed stream: The concept is not the problem. The infrastructure that implements it is. Smart city technology, digital IDs, carbon tracking, movement data—the 15-minute city is the physical layer. The surveillance is the digital layer.

GAP: The 15-minute city is an urban planning concept. It is not a containment grid. But the infrastructure required to implement it—data collection, mobility tracking, centralized planning—is the same infrastructure required for surveillance. The concept is not the plan. It is the excuse for the infrastructure.
