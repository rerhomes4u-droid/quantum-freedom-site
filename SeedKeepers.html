01The Evidence: What Happened in Campoalegre

Documented
The Documentary That Started the Movement

In 2013, filmmaker Victoria Solano released "9.70," a documentary showing ICA officials, escorted by riot police, confiscating and destroying 62 tons of rice seed from farmers in Campoalegre, Huila. The farmers had saved the best part of their harvest to plant again. The police treated it as illegal [citation:23].

The video went viral. Within 20 days, half a million people had seen it. It became the visual evidence of what Resolution 970 was doing to small farmers. The ICA tried to have it removed from a film festival, claiming it "contained lies." The festival committee refused [citation:25].

The record shows the scale: Between 2010 and 2011, the ICA confiscated 1,167,225 kilograms of non-certified seed, mostly rice, but also beans, maize, potato, and wheat. The next year, the amount more than doubled .

The Court Ruling That Proves the System Is Broken

The Constitutional Court found genetic contamination of native maize in indigenous territories in five regions of the country [citation:2].

The Court found that authorities had no legal or technical mechanisms to protect native seeds from transgenic crops [citation:6].

The Court ordered the Ministry of Agriculture to establish legal mechanisms and technical evaluations to control and avoid genetic contamination of criollo maize, and to implement a participatory regulatory framework for the protection of native and criollo seeds [citation:2].

The Court granted "inter comunis" effects—meaning the ruling protects not just the indigenous communities who filed the case, but any indigenous population in the same conditions [citation:2].

WARNING: Ruling T-247/23 is a tutela ruling. It protects indigenous communities. It does not automatically protect every farmer in the country. The seed network must use the ruling as precedent, not as a universal shield. Each member needs their own legal protection. The government has been slow to comply. As of August 2025, the implementation process had "not advanced within the timeframe established by the court, due to the delaying obstacles and the lack of political decision" [citation:6].

02The Controversial Claim: The Government Rewrote the Rules to Steal Sovereignty

Contested
What the Record Shows and What It Does Not

The claim: Resolution 970 was a requirement of the US-Colombia Free Trade Agreement, imposed to benefit multinational seed corporations like Monsanto, Dupont, and Syngenta.

What the record supports: The documentary "9.70" and its director, Victoria Solano, explicitly link Resolution 970 to the FTA and to UPOV 91, the international convention on plant variety protection [citation:19][citation:27]. Colombia signed Law 1518 in 2012 to accede to UPOV 91, but the Constitutional Court struck it down because it lacked prior consultation with indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities [citation:9]. The FTA with the US continued to impact domestic policy [citation:9].

What the record does not support: The ICA's official position is that Resolution 970 does not target native seeds and is not related to the FTA. The ICA's manager said the resolution "is directed at improved materials, not native ones, because that is not our competence" [citation:27]. The ICA also said the confiscated rice was contaminated and not fit for human consumption, according to Invima [citation:20].

The gap: The documentary links 970 to the FTA. The ICA denies it. The Constitutional Court struck down the UPOV 91 law. The record contains both the claim and the denial. The reader decides which is more credible.

The Seed Guardians of Colombia

The network exists and is organized. It organizes in territorial nodes. Seed care is a family activity that contributes to the collective. Women are central in selection, care, conservation, and oral transmission of practices. Indigenous peoples contribute traditional knowledge tied to cosmology and ecological calendars.

Community seed houses qualify conservation techniques, guarantee availability and storage, and run transgenic contamination tests to prevent circulation.

The Popayán Manifesto (2025): Indigenous, Afro, and peasant organizations declared that native and criollo seeds are common goods of the peoples and must remain free from all forms of appropriation.
