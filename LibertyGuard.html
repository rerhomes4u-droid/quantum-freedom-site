🔒 Fourth Amendment
Protects the people — your person, house, papers, and effects — against unreasonable seizures. Your body is a person. An arrest is a seizure. This is your shield.
🗣️ First Amendment
The right to record police in public is protected. Illinois law explicitly prohibits officers from hindering a person recording an officer performing public duties. Keep recording — do not physically interfere.
⏳ Prompt Magistrate (Rule 5 / McLaughlin)
You must be brought before a judge for a probable cause determination without unnecessary delay. The Supreme Court's benchmark: 48 hours. Detention beyond that without a hearing can support a Fourth Amendment claim.
⚖️ Fifth Amendment
Right to remain silent. You must state it out loud: "I invoke my right to remain silent." Then stop talking. Ask for a lawyer.
📜 Illinois Expungement
If charges were dropped or dismissed, you are generally eligible for expungement with no waiting period under Illinois law (20 ILCS 2630/5.2). The arresting agency and State Police must destroy or return the records.
❌ Myth: "I can refuse fingerprinting and mugshots."
After a lawful arrest, booking procedures are standard. Courts treat fingerprinting as a minimal intrusion. Refusing can add obstruction charges. Compliance is not consent — you still challenge the arrest later.
❌ Myth: "48 hours means they must release me."
48 hours is a benchmark, not an automatic release trigger. Detention beyond it without a probable cause hearing can support a claim — but it is not automatic false imprisonment.
❌ Myth: "State citizen / sovereign citizen status wins in court."
Federal courts, including the Seventh Circuit (Illinois), have summarily rejected these arguments [citation:16][citation:30]. They will not protect you in a §1983 lawsuit. The Fourth Amendment's protection of "the people" and "persons" is your real ground.
❌ Myth: "Expungement removes everything from the internet."
Expungement cleans the legal record. It does not automatically remove news articles or mugshots from Google or private sites. You must pursue publisher removal separately.

⚖️ What §1983 Requires
To state a claim under 42 U.S.C. §1983, you must allege:

1. A person acted under color of state law (police officers qualify) [citation:1][citation:2].
2. That conduct deprived you of a federal constitutional right (e.g., Fourth Amendment — unreasonable seizure / prolonged detention without probable cause hearing).
🛡️ Qualified Immunity — The Real Wall
Officers are shielded from liability unless they violated a clearly established constitutional right that a reasonable officer would have known [citation:11]. This is the biggest hurdle. Your timeline log — especially detention duration before magistrate — is your best evidence.
⏰ Deadlines — Do Not Miss
Notice of Claim: Some jurisdictions require notice within 90–180 days.
Statute of Limitations: For §1983, courts borrow the state's personal injury statute of limitations [citation:12]. In Illinois, that is 2 years.
