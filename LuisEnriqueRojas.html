👤 WHO IS LUIS ENRIQUE ROJAS?

Luis Enrique Rojas Cuéllar is a petroleum engineer with over 27 years of experience in the energy sector. He served as president of Hocol, a subsidiary of Colombia's state oil company Ecopetrol, from May 2024 to April 2025 [citation:6].

He holds a master's degree in Business Administration and has held executive positions in oil companies both in Colombia and abroad [citation:6].

But in 2025, he made a decision that would change everything. He refused to be silent.

⚖️ HIS TESTIMONY

Luis Enrique Rojas is the star witness against Ricardo Roa, president of Ecopetrol, who has been formally accused of influence trafficking [citation:2][citation:14]. His testimony could determine the future of Colombia's most important company.

"I could have stayed silent, given in to the pressures and protected my position. Or I could defend my principles, protect a company I considered the patrimony of Colombians, and act according to the law. I chose the second."

— Luis Enrique Rojas

"They sought to discredit me. They sought to pressure me. They sought, according to various sources, to build a corruption case against me. They couldn't do it because I acted with transparency and within the framework of the law."

They can be attacked, persecuted, or pointed out, but they retain something no one can take away: the certainty of having done what is right."

His denunciation helped uncover a corruption scandal that allegedly involved:

  • Pressures to hand over a lucrative gas contract to a company linked to a close associate of Roa
  • A luxury apartment (the "Penthouse 901") purchased below market value by Roa
  • Alleged meetings in Spain and Italy to influence electoral magistrates

🚨 THE THREATS

Rojas has been subjected to a campaign of intimidation since his testimony became known. The Fiscalía (Colombia's prosecutor's office) has confirmed the threats and is evaluating his protection [citation:2][citation:18].

📞 "Five Days to Leave the Country"

On February 28, 2024, Rojas received a call from a number in the Netherlands. A distorted voice told him: "If you keep testifying, we'll kill you and your family." He was given five days to leave Colombia [citation:13][citation:17].

📡 Surveillance and Wiretapping

An intelligence sweep of his office found microphones in August 2024. His security detail was infiltrated. His phone and computer were intervened. His location was being tracked and reported to third parties [citation:1][citation:5][citation:10].

🎯 Armed Interception

On December 6, 2024, two armed individuals intercepted his vehicle. When they realized he wasn't inside, they fled [citation:5][citation:10].

🔫 Contract to Kill

Rojas received a call from someone claiming to be a guerrilla commander who admitted he had been hired to assassinate him [citation:13].

📱 Disinformation Campaign

He has been subjected to campaigns to discredit him, to frame him for corruption, and to destroy his reputation [citation:5][citation:9].

🔍 WHAT HE EXPOSED

💰 The Gaxi Contract

Rojas testified that he was pressured to hand over the Chuchupa-Ballena regasification project to Gaxi S.A. ESP, a company linked to Juan Guillermo Mancera — a close associate of Roa. He refused, citing technical and financial reasons [citation:9][citation:17].

🏠 The Penthouse 901

Roa allegedly purchased a luxury apartment in Bogotá for $1.8 billion pesos when it was valued at $2.727 billion — a discount of nearly $1 billion. Mancera is accused of financing the purchase [citation:17].

🗳️ Electoral Interference

An anonymous report Rojas received alleged meetings in Spain and Italy where $10 million per magistrate was offered to influence a CNE ruling related to the Petro campaign [citation:9][citation:13].

🌍 International Testimony

Rojas has presented evidence to U.S. authorities, including the SEC and DOJ, because Ecopetrol trades on the New York Stock Exchange. This international dimension has increased pressure on the company [citation:17].

⚠️ THE PATTERN

Luis Enrique Rojas is not the first Colombian who spoke truth to power and was threatened with death. According to the United Nations, 972 human rights defenders were killed in Colombia between 2016 and 2025 — an average of nearly 100 per year [citation:4][citation:8].

The UN has warned that Colombia remains "one of the deadliest places on earth to be a human rights defender" [citation:12]. The impunity rate for these crimes is extremely high. Corruption and weak institutions are cited as contributing factors [citation:8].

Luis Enrique Rojas is the latest name on a list that is already too long. His case is a test: will Colombia protect a witness who risked everything to expose corruption? Or will he become another statistic?
