▣ REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION
THE PROCEDURAL TRAP

THE TRAP

Requests for Admission are not questions. They are statements that the other side must either admit, deny, or object to. If they fail to respond within 30 days, the statements are admitted as true.

This is the trap. Most people don't know it exists. Most people don't use it. Most people lose cases they could have won because they didn't know they could force the other side to admit the truth — or commit perjury.

"If they fail to respond, the facts are admitted. If they lie, they commit perjury. If they object without justification, the court can order an answer and award expenses."

▣ THE RULE
RULE 36, SCRCP
Requests for Admission. A party may serve on any other party a written request to admit the truth of any matters within the scope of discovery. The request must be answered within 30 days. Failure to answer constitutes admission.
RULE 36(c), SCRCP
The 20-Limit Rule. A party may serve no more than twenty (20) requests for admission on any other party, including subparts, unless the court grants leave to serve more. This is a critical limit. If you serve more than 20, the other side can object and the court may strike the entire set.
RULE 36(b), SCRCP
Effect of Admission. Any matter admitted is conclusively established for the purpose of the pending action. This means the admitted fact cannot be challenged later in the case. It is a binding admission.
⚠ IMPORTANT: You must serve exactly 20 requests per party. Not 21. Not 19. Exactly 20. If you serve fewer, you are wasting an opportunity. If you serve more, you are giving the other side an easy objection.
▣ THE STRATEGY
STEP 1
Identify the parties. Who are the people who have wronged you? Who are the witnesses? Who are the agency workers? Who are the supervisors? List them. You can serve 20 requests on each party.
STEP 2
Identify the facts. What do you need to establish? What do you need them to admit? What would hurt them most if it were admitted as true? What would they be forced to either admit or commit perjury on?
STEP 3
Draft the requests. Each request must be a statement that can be admitted or denied. Not a question. Not a narrative. A statement. "Admit that you have never contacted the Petitioner." "Admit that E.B.H. was never in the custody of the State." "Admit that you never provided the documents."
STEP 4
Serve the requests. Certified mail. Return receipt requested. And/or email. Keep the receipts. The 30-day clock starts on the date of service.
STEP 5
Wait 30 days. If they respond, check their answers. If they lie, that is perjury. If they object, that is a basis for a motion to compel. If they fail to respond, the facts are admitted.
STEP 6
File the admissions. If they fail to respond, file a notice with the court stating that the facts are admitted. If they lied, file a motion for sanctions. If they objected, file a motion to compel.

▣ THE PRO TIPS
TIP 1
Make each request specific. "Admit that you never contacted Nolvia" is better than "Admit that you failed to do your job." Specific facts are harder to deny. Specific facts are harder to explain away.
TIP 2
Make each request verifiable. If you can prove it, make them admit it. If they deny it, you have proof of perjury. If they admit it, you have a binding admission. Either way, you win.
TIP 3
Make each request matter. Don't waste a request on something irrelevant. Every request should be a brick in the wall of your case. Each one should build on the last.
TIP 4
Serve on every party. Each party gets their own 20. The DSS caseworker gets 20. The supervisor gets 20. The abuser gets 20. The GAL gets 20. The list goes on. You can serve on all of them.
TIP 5
Calendar the 30-day deadline. The day you serve is Day 0. Day 30 is the deadline. If they fail to respond, file the admissions on Day 31.
