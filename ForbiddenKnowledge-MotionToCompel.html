▣ MOTION TO COMPEL
GET THE EVIDENCE

GET THE EVIDENCE

If you ask for evidence and the other side refuses to produce it, you file a Motion to Compel. This is the tool that forces them to hand it over.

The 911 call. The dashcam footage. The incident reports. The communications. Whatever it is, if it is relevant and they have it, they have to produce it.

"If the motion is granted, the court must require the non-producing party to pay the moving party's reasonable expenses, unless the opposition was substantially justified."

▣ THE RULE
RULE 26, SCRCP
General Provisions Governing Discovery. Parties may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, which is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action.
RULE 34, SCRCP
Production of Documents. A party may request the production of documents, records, and other tangible things within the scope of Rule 26.
RULE 37, SCRCP
Failure to Make Discovery: Sanctions. If a party fails to respond to discovery requests, the requesting party may file a motion to compel.
RULE 37(a)(4), SCRCP
Expenses. If the motion is granted, the court shall require the party whose conduct necessitated the motion to pay the moving party's reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, unless the court finds that the opposition was substantially justified.
⚠ IMPORTANT: You must request the evidence first. You cannot file a Motion to Compel for something you never asked for. Serve a written discovery request. Wait for the response. If they refuse or fail to respond, then file the motion.
▣ THE STRATEGY
STEP 1
Identify the evidence. What do you need? The 911 call. The body camera footage. The incident report. The text messages. The emails. The medical records. Whatever it is, identify it specifically.
STEP 2
Serve a written discovery request. Pursuant to Rule 34, SCRCP. Request the production of the specific documents. Give them a deadline. 30 days is standard.
STEP 3
Wait for the response. If they produce the documents, great. If they refuse, if they object, if they fail to respond, you have grounds for a Motion to Compel.
STEP 4
File the motion. Cite Rule 37. Explain what you requested, when you requested it, and what they did or didn't do. Ask the court to compel production and award expenses.
STEP 5
Attend the hearing. Most motions to compel are decided without a hearing. But if there is one, show up. Be prepared. Be specific. Be polite.

▣ THE PRO TIPS
TIP 1
Be specific. "The 911 call from October 18, 2025" is better than "all records related to the incident." Specific requests are harder to refuse.
TIP 2
Keep the receipts. If you served the request by certified mail, keep the green card. If by email, keep the sent email. You need to prove you asked.
TIP 3
File promptly. If they miss the deadline, file the motion. Don't wait. The longer you wait, the more it looks like you're not serious.
TIP 4
Ask for expenses. If you win the motion, ask for expenses. This is the sanction. It is the incentive for them to comply next time.
TIP 5
Be polite. Don't attack the other side in the motion. Don't accuse them of lying. Just state the facts: you asked, they didn't produce, you filed the motion.
