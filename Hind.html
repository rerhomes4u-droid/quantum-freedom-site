Her Name Was Hind

She was five years old. She was trapped in a car with her dead family. She called for help. For three hours, she begged. No one came. This is her voice.

🎙️ HER VOICE

This is the actual recording of Hind Rajab's call to the Palestinian Red Crescent Society on January 29, 2024. She was five years old. She was alone. She was surrounded by the bodies of her family.

"Come take me. Will you come and take me? I'm so scared, please come!" — Hind Rajab, age 5, on the phone with emergency dispatchers
"Please don't leave me. I am afraid of the dark." — Hind Rajab, as night fell and the tank approached
"The tank is very close. And it's moving." — Hind Rajab, describing the Israeli tank approaching the car

The full recording is available on the Palestinian Red Crescent Society website and has been incorporated into the Oscar-nominated film "The Voice of Hind Rajab."

📖 What Happened

On January 29, 2024, five-year-old Hind Rajab was fleeing Gaza City with her uncle's family. They were trying to reach safety after the Israeli military ordered residents of Tel al-Hawa to evacuate. Their black Kia Picanto came under Israeli fire.

Hind's uncle, aunt, and three cousins were killed. Her 15-year-old cousin Layan was wounded. Layan called the Palestinian Red Crescent Society. The call ended with the sound of gunfire and Layan's screams.

When dispatchers called back, Hind answered. She was the only survivor. For over three hours, she stayed on the phone with dispatcher Rana Faqih. She described the tank moving closer. She asked for help. She said she was afraid of the dark.

An ambulance was dispatched after coordination with the Israeli military. Paramedics Yousef Zeino and Ahmed al-Madhoun were sent to rescue her. They were shelled. Both were killed.

Twelve days later, Hind's body was found in the car, alongside six family members. The car had 335 bullet holes. The ambulance was destroyed. The paramedics were dead.

⚖️ Israel's Admission

On August 19, 2026 — more than two and a half years after Hind's death — the Israeli military finally admitted that its forces fired on the car carrying Hind and her family, and on the ambulance sent to rescue her.

"Due to alleged failures in the coordination of the movement of the Palestine Red Crescent ambulance, it has been decided to initiate a criminal investigation."

— Israeli Defense Forces statement, August 19, 2026

The admission came after Israel initially denied any troops were in the area. It later said it had "conducted raids on terror targets" in Tel al-Hawa. The military deleted that press release from its website.

"We were surprised by the statement issued by the Israeli army. But for us this is not an admission but rather an attempt by the Israeli army to justify to the world how it committed the crime and how they executed Hind."

— Hind Rajab's grandmother, also named Hind Rajab

"They acknowledged the Hind incident because it was documented, but many more crimes have occurred in Gaza that have not been documented."

— Hind Rajab's grandmother

🔥 Why This Matters

Hind Rajab was not a combatant. She was not a threat. She was a five-year-old girl who wanted to be a doctor. She was trapped in a car with her dead family, and she called for help.

Her voice was recorded. The world heard it. And the world did nothing.

This is not an isolated incident. According to Gaza's health ministry, more than 73,400 Palestinians have been killed since October 2023. Women and children constitute about half of those deaths. But numbers become statistics. Statistics become forgettable. Hind's voice makes it real.

"All I remember is her voice pleading, 'Mama, I'm scared. Mama, I'm hungry. Mama, I'm hurt.' Her voice keeps repeating in my ears."

— Wissam Hamada, Hind's mother

"I wanted my daughter to be a doctor. She was so smart. She was so beautiful. Now my home is filled with the loudest silence."

— Wissam Hamada, Hind's mother
