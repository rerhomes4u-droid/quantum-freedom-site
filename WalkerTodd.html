The Two Types of Money

Walker Todd explains the critical distinction that most people don't understand:

Secret Knowledge

Money of Exchange: Physical currency, gold, silver - what most people think of as "real money".

Money of Account: Bank credit, ledger entries, promises to pay - this is what actually powers the modern economy.

Banks operate primarily with money of account (credit), not money of exchange. When you take out a loan, the bank doesn't lend you existing money; it creates new credit on its books.

"In a fractional reserve banking system, a comparatively small amount of money of exchange may support a vastly larger quantity of business transactions denominated in money of account."

Fractional Reserve Banking

Banks only need to keep a fraction (typically 10%) of deposits as reserves. They can lend out the rest, creating new money in the process.

Credit Creation

When banks make loans, they create deposits (credit) out of thin air. These deposits become "money" in the economy.

Lawful Money vs Legal Tender

"Lawful money" traditionally meant gold/silver. "Legal tender" is whatever the government declares (today: Federal Reserve notes).

How Banks Really Operate

Based on Todd's affidavit and GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles):

  1. When you sign a promissory note, the bank records it as an asset on their books.
  2. Simultaneously, they create a matching liability (your deposit account).
  3. No actual "money" changes hands - only accounting entries.
  4. The bank monetizes your signature, creating credit that functions as money.

The Big Secret

In many loan transactions, the customer might actually be the lender and the bank might be the borrower. Your promissory note becomes an asset for the bank, while the credit they give you is their liability.

Financial System "Cheat Codes"

Practical insights from Walker Todd's affidavit that can change how you interact with the financial system:

Cheat Code #1: Understand Credit Creation

When you borrow, you're not taking someone else's money - you're getting newly created credit. Negotiate from this position of knowledge.

Cheat Code #2: The Promissory Note is Power

Your signed note is a valuable asset to the bank. Recognize your leverage in loan negotiations.

Cheat Code #3: Money of Account Rules

Focus on building creditworthiness and relationships, not just accumulating cash. In modern banking, credit (money of account) is more powerful than cash (money of exchange).

Cheat Code #4: The Double-Entry Revelation

Every loan creates both an asset and liability. Understand what's being created on both sides of the transaction.

Advanced Insights

Hidden Banking Mechanism

Banks don't lend reserves or deposits. They create new deposits when they make loans. Reserves settle transactions between banks AFTER the loan is made.

The "Lawful Money" Trap

Loan documents often mention "lawful money" but banks deal in credit. This creates an asymmetry where you promise to repay in "lawful money" but receive only bank credit.

True Consideration

When a bank creates credit from your promissory note, there may be a failure of consideration in the legal sense - the bank isn't actually lending you pre-existing assets.

Practical Implementation in Daily Life

1. Loan Negotiations

When applying for a loan, understand that the bank creates the money from your promise to repay. Use this knowledge to negotiate better terms. Ask questions about how the transaction will be booked on their accounting system.

2. Credit Building Strategy

Since the system runs on credit (money of account), focus on building impeccable credit history. A high credit score gives you access to the credit creation mechanism.

3. Business Financing

When seeking business loans, prepare your promissory note as if it were a financial instrument (because it is). Present it professionally, understanding its value to the bank.

4. Legal Awareness

Review loan documents for "lawful money" clauses. Understand that you're likely receiving credit, not "lawful money." This awareness can be important in disputes.

5. Financial Literacy

Teach others about the distinction between money of exchange and money of account. This fundamental understanding changes how people view debt, banking, and money.
