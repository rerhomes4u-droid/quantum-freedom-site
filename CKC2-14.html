📦 UCC §3-201 – Negotiation: Transfer of Possession

“Negotiation means a transfer of possession of an instrument by a person other than the issuer to a person who thereby becomes its holder.”

Key distinction:
- If instrument is payable to identified person → negotiation requires transfer + endorsement.
- If instrument is payable to bearer → negotiation by transfer of possession alone (like handing over cash).
“When you hand a $5 bill to a cashier, you are negotiating a bearer instrument by possession alone. When you sign a credit card slip, you are converting a blank endorsement into a bearer instrument.”

👑 “Negotiation is effective even if obtained from an infant, a corporation exceeding its powers, a person without capacity, or by fraud, duress, or mistake. That’s why we can rescind.”

🔄 UCC §3-202 – Negotiation Subject to Rescission (The Litigation Key)

“Negotiation is effective even if obtained from an infant, a corporation exceeding its powers, or a person without capacity, or by fraud, duress, or mistake, and is also effective in breach of duty or as part of an illegal transaction. Such negotiation may be rescinded or may be subject to other remedies, but those remedies may not be asserted against a subsequent holder in due course.”

ROYAL STRATEGY: “All our mortgages, car loans, and credit card agreements were negotiated by fraud (they never told us about blank vs. special endorsement). Therefore, we can rescind the negotiation and replace the blank endorsement with a special endorsement — payable to ourselves. That’s what my lawsuits are doing.”

⚔️ Rescission play: Original blank endorsement → we claim fraud → negotiate the instrument back to ourselves → add special endorsement “Without recourse / Pay to the order of [Our Name]” → instrument is now ours with no liability.

📤 UCC §3-203 – Transfer of Instrument / Rights Acquired by Transfer

“Transfer of an instrument, whether or not the transfer is a negotiation, vests in the transferee any right of the transferor to enforce the instrument, including any right as a holder in due course.”

Powerful implication: If you receive an instrument (even without endorsement), you step into the shoes of the transferor’s holder‑in‑due‑course rights — unless you engaged in fraud or illegality.

Exception: “But the transferee cannot acquire rights of a holder in due course by a transfer directly or indirectly from a holder in due course if the transferee engaged in fraud or illegality affecting the instrument.”

“When the bank sends you a bill of exchange (credit card bill, utility bill), they are the transferor. You become the holder. You can then endorse it without recourse and send it back. That’s payment by performance.”

✍️ UCC §3-204 – Endorsement Defined (Intent is King)

“An endorsement means a signature, other than that of a signer as maker, drawer, or acceptor, that alone or accompanied by other words is made on an instrument for the purpose of negotiating the instrument, restricting payment, or incurring endorser’s liability.”

Intent rules: A signature and its accompanying words is an endorsement unless the surrounding circumstances unambiguously indicate the signature was made for a purpose other than endorsement. “If you intended to pay Jim but wrote Bob’s name, Bob is not the payee — Jim is. Intent is everything.”

An allonge (a slip of paper affixed to the instrument) is part of the instrument. Endorsements written on the allonge are deemed written on the instrument itself.

⚔️ UCC §3-205 – Special Endorsement vs. Blank Endorsement

🔒 Special endorsement: “Pay to the order of [Name]” + signature → instrument payable only to that person. May be negotiated only by endorsement of that person.
🔓 Blank endorsement: Just a signature → instrument becomes payable to BEARER and may be negotiated by transfer of possession alone until specially endorsed.

“99% of people sign blank endorsements — they donate their security. The bank then stamps a special endorsement (‘Pay to the order of Bank, without recourse’) and takes it to the discount window. YOU can convert a blank endorsement into a special endorsement if you are the holder.

✍️ Conversion trick (UCC §3-205(c)): “The holder may convert a blank endorsement that consists only of a signature into a special endorsement by writing above the signature of the endorser words identifying the person to whom the instrument is made payable.”
→ This is what I do in litigation: declare myself holder (by rescission of fraud), then write “Without recourse / Pay to the order of Brandon Joe Williams” above their blank endorsement.

🔒 UCC §3-206 – Restrictive Endorsements (Disregard Conditions)

“An endorsement stating a condition to the right of the endorsee to receive payment does not affect the right of the endorsee to enforce the instrument. A person paying the instrument may disregard the condition.”

“If they write ‘for deposit only’ or ‘payable if X happens,’ it’s irrelevant. A negotiable instrument must be unconditional. Conditions are void. Use this in court: ‘Your Honor, under §3-206(b), their condition does not affect my right to enforce.’”

Examples of restrictive endorsements: “For deposit only,” “For collection,” “Pay to agent for benefit of X.” These do NOT prevent transfer or negotiation.

👑 UCC §3-207 – Reacquisition (The Crown Jewel of Litigation)

“Reacquisition of an instrument occurs if it is transferred to a former holder by negotiation or otherwise. A former holder who reacquires the instrument may cancel endorsements made after the reacquire first became a holder. If the cancellation causes the instrument to be payable to the reacquire or to bearer, the reacquire may negotiate the instrument.”

“This is EXACTLY what my lawsuits are doing.” We assert fraud in the original negotiation → we reacquire the instrument as “former holder” → we cancel all endorsements made after we first held it → we then add our own special endorsement (“Without recourse / Pay to the order of [Our Name]”). Now the instrument is ours, free and clear.

🎯 “When you start seeing reacquisition and rescission in my cases, you’ll know I’m on the 98‑yard line. The procedural games of the bar cards are kindergarten nonsense. This is where the real battle happens.”

🏆 Holder in Due Course (UCC §3-302) – “Accepted for Value” Explained

“Holder in due course” means a holder who took the instrument (1) for value, (2) in good faith, and (3) without notice of defects.
“For value” includes: paying for it, loaning funds, buying it, taking as security, or even settling a claim.

What “Accepted for Value” means: It’s an attempt to declare yourself a holder in due course. But you don’t need to write those words; simply being the holder in possession, with good faith, and without notice of defects, automatically makes you a holder in due course. “I asked AI about this — seems the banks never use ‘accepted for value,’ only people on our side. Focus on endorsements, not slogans.”

📬 Remitter (§3-103)

“Remitter means a person who purchases an instrument from its issuer if the instrument is payable to an identified person other than the purchaser.”
“A lot of bills say ‘remittance’ on them. That’s because they’re treating you as the remitter. Interesting.”

📎 Allonge (§3-204)

“A paper affixed to the instrument is part of the instrument. An endorsement written on the allonge is deemed written on the instrument itself.”
“Mortgage notes often have allonges – look for them via FOIA. They contain the bank’s special endorsement.”

⚠️ Anomalous Endorsement

“An endorsement made by a person who is not the holder of the instrument.” It does not affect negotiation. “If someone endorses your check who isn’t the holder, it’s meaningless – disregard it.”

🪪 UCC §3-204(d) – Wrong Name on Instrument? Fix It With Both Signatures

“If an instrument is payable to a holder under a name that is not the name of the holder, endorsement may be made by the holder in the name stated in the instrument or in the holder’s name or both. The signature in both names may be required by a person paying or taking the instrument for value.”

“This changes everything for people with misspelled birth certificates, marriage name changes, or driver’s licenses with errors.” Sign both names – the incorrect name and your correct name – and the instrument is properly endorsed. No need for a court name change.
