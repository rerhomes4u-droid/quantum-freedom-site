02 Electromagnetic Hypersensitivity & "Microwave Syndrome"

The Claim

Non-thermal microwave radiation from cell towers, Wi-Fi, and 5G causes a documented condition called electromagnetic hypersensitivity — headaches, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, heart palpitations, cognitive dysfunction.

Record: Contested, not dismissed Suppressed: The people who suffer are called psychosomatic
The Record

The condition is documented in peer-reviewed literature. Lennart Hardell and colleagues published "Summary of seven Swedish case reports on the microwave syndrome associated with 5G radiofrequency radiation" in Reviews on Environmental Health (2024) [citation:7][citation:20].

The cases included 16 people aged 4 to 83 who developed symptoms within a short time after 5G base stations were installed close to their dwellings. Symptoms included headaches, tinnitus, dizziness, anxiety, and heart arrhythmias. Symptoms resolved when they moved away from the exposure [citation:7].

Hardell has also published critiques of the ICNIRP limits, arguing they protect only against thermal effects and ignore non-thermal biological effects [citation:7].

The condition has a name in the literature — electromagnetic hypersensitivity — but in the clinic it is often treated as a psychological problem, not an environmental one.

The question the fact-checkers do not answer: If the symptoms are psychosomatic, why do they resolve when the person moves away from the tower? Why do the case reports cluster geographically around 5G installations? Why are children among the reported cases?

The suppressed claim: The grid is making people sick, and the people who are sick are being gaslit because admitting the cause would require admitting the grid is not safe.

03 The Blood-Brain Barrier & Microwave Radiation

The Claim

Microwave radiation increases the permeability of the blood-brain barrier, allowing particles that would normally be kept out — including aluminum and heavy metals — to cross into the brain.

Record: Thermal effect confirmed, non-thermal contested Suppressed: The human data does not exist
The Record

Thermal effects are confirmed. A 2010 review by Rianne Stam concluded that "exposure to levels of radiofrequency electromagnetic fields (EMF) that increase brain temperature by more than 1°C can reversibly increase the permeability of the BBB for macromolecules" [citation:14].

Non-thermal effects are not supported by the balance of evidence. The same review states: "The balance of experimental evidence does not support an effect of 'non-thermal' radiofrequency fields with microwave and mobile phone frequencies on BBB permeability" [citation:14].

A 1982 study found no significant increase after correcting for thermal effects [citation:6].

The Suppressed

The human data does not exist. The same 2010 review that dismisses non-thermal effects also states: "Studies on the potential effect of EMF exposure on permeability of the BBB in humans are virtually absent" [citation:14].

That is the gap. The official side says "no non-thermal effect" based on animal studies. The human studies have not been done. The question of what happens to the human brain under chronic, low-level, non-thermal exposure — the kind of exposure that is now universal — has not been answered.

The suppressed claim: The reason the human studies have not been done is that the answer would be inconvenient. The grid is too profitable to question.

  • PubMed: Stam, "Electromagnetic fields and the blood-brain barrier" (2010) pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20550949/ The official review. It confirms thermal effects. It dismisses non-thermal effects. It also confirms that human studies are "virtually absent." The gap is in the official record.
  • PubMed: Ward et al., "Measurement of blood-brain barrier permeation in rats during exposure to 2450-MHz microwaves" (1982) pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7126285/ The animal study. After correcting for thermal effects, no significant increase. But it was rats, not humans. The human question remains open.
GAP: The official side says "no non-thermal effect." But the human studies have not been done. The animal studies have methodological limits. The gap is not "the science is settled" — it is "the science has not been done on humans, and the exposure is now universal."
