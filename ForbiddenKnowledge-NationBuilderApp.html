Step One: The Architecture

You are not building a trust. You are building a nation. A three-tier structure. A waterfall. A promissory note. A credit line. A legacy.

Before you enter a single item, you must understand what you are building and what it can and cannot do.

The Three Tiers

Tier One: The Revocable Living Trust. Created during your lifetime. You are the grantor, the trustee, the beneficiary. You control it. It holds your assets. It avoids probate. It provides incapacity planning.

Tier Two: The Irrevocable Family Trust. The beneficiary of the revocable trust is not a person. It is another trust. When you die (or a triggering event occurs), the assets waterfall into the family trust. No probate. No individual tax liability on the waterfall.

Tier Three: The Business Trust. Optional. For business assets and intellectual property. The business trust holds the IP. The corporation operates. The LLC holds the DBA. You assign a percentage — less than 50% — to the revocable trust.

The Mechanism: The Promissory Note

You inventory everything you own. You assign the original value. You add it up. That total becomes the value of a promissory note that the trust creates to purchase those items from you.

The trust owes you. You hold the promissory note. As the trust generates income, you take non-taxable distributions off that note because the trust owes you.

What this app is NOT: It is not legal advice. It does not replace an attorney. It does not guarantee the bank will lend. It does not make self-dealing legal. If you create a false instrument, present it, and it is not backed by real value, that is a criminal offense. This app generates the blueprint. The professionals execute it.
What a trust cannot do: It cannot protect you from criminal prosecution. It cannot work if you never fund it. No trust takes effect until it is funded. It cannot be administered by you alone if it is irrevocable. It cannot be used to hide assets from a pending creditor — that is a fraudulent transfer.

Step Two: The Manifest (Inventory)

Walk through your home. Every room. Every closet. Every drawer. List every item. Assign the original value — not the depreciated value, not the resale value. The value you declare. You are the sovereign. You set the value.

This inventory is the manifest. It is the basis of the promissory note. It is the foundation of the nation.

Step Four: The Revocable Living Trust

The revocable trust is the vessel. It holds the assets. It receives the promissory note. It waterfalls into the family trust at death.

Step Five: The Bank Package

This is what you carry to the institution. Not to a branch. To the private wealth or trust department in a major office building. You speak their language. You are not fishing for information. You are directing them.

What Goes in the Package
  • The signed promissory note (from Step Three)
  • The inventory manifest (from Step Two)
  • The certificate of trust (the short instrument the attorney prepares — this app gives you the structure)
  • The trust agreement (the full document the attorney prepares)
  • Your ID and the trust's EIN (if the trust has one — a revocable trust typically uses your SSN; an irrevocable trust uses its own EIN, and you should not be the responsible party)
  • A cover letter requesting a discussion of a credit line against the trust's assets

The One-Year Timeline

Month One: Complete the manifest. Sign it. Date it.

Month Two: Consult an attorney. Draft the revocable trust and the family trust. The attorney prepares the certificate of trust.

Months Five through Twelve: Fund the trust. Acquire the life insurance policy naming the family trust as beneficiary. Refine the structure. Train your successor.
