📄 UCC §3-104 – What Is a Negotiable Instrument?

“A negotiable instrument is an unconditional promise or order to pay a fixed amount of money, payable to bearer or to order, on demand or at a definite time.”

Two types:
- Promissory note (promise to pay) – e.g., mortgage, car loan, IOU.
- Bill of exchange / draft / check (order to pay) – e.g., utility bill, check.
“All you need to ask: Is it an unconditional promise? Or an unconditional order? That’s it.”

⚖️ UCC §3-106 – Conditional vs. Unconditional (My Case 1 Lesson)

“A promise or order is UNCONDITIONAL unless it states an express condition to payment, or is subject to/governed by another record.”
“In Case 1, the federal judge dismissed because my promissory notes had express conditions. She was right about that part. I learned the hard way.”

Lesson: If you are drafting an instrument, strip out all conditions. “Pay $1,000” – unconditional. “Pay $1,000 if the roof doesn’t leak” – conditional (not negotiable).

💰 UCC §3-112 – Interest (Literally Whatever You Want)

“Interest may be stated as a fixed or variable amount, fixed or variable rate, and may require reference to information not contained in the instrument. If the amount cannot be ascertained, interest is payable at the judgment rate.”

“Interest is arbitrary. Write 0%. Or write 100%. As long as both parties agree (or you are the holder), it’s valid.”

✍️ §3-114 – Contradictory Terms

“Typewritten terms prevail over printed. Handwritten terms prevail over both. Words prevail over numbers.”

🖋️ UCC §3-205 – Blank Endorsement (Donation) vs. Special Endorsement (Claim)

🔓 Blank endorsement: just a signature – instrument becomes payable to BEARER (anyone who holds it). This is what 99% of people do – they abandoned their security.
🔒 Special endorsement: “Pay to the order of [name]” + signature – instrument is payable only to that person.

“When you sign a mortgage with a blank endorsement, you donate the note to the bank. They take it, stamp ‘without recourse’ and their name, swap it at the Federal Reserve, and loan you your own money back. Always use special endorsement.”

👑 UCC §3-109 – Payable to Bearer (My Steampunk Royal Moment)

“A promise or order is payable to bearer if it states it is payable to bearer, to the order of bearer, or otherwise indicates that the person in possession is entitled to payment.”

🏆 Holder in Due Course (UCC §3-302) – Ultimate Power

A holder in due course takes the instrument free from most defenses (except real defenses like fraud, illegality). To qualify: (1) holder for value, (2) in good faith, (3) without notice of defects.

“When you receive an instrument and give value (anything of value, even a promise), you become a holder in due course. Then you can enforce it free from their excuses. This is how banks win. Now you can too.”

⏳ UCC §3-118 – Statute of Limitations (6 years / 10 years)

- Note payable at definite time: 6 years after due date.
- Note payable on demand: 6 years after demand; if no demand ever made and no payment for 10 continuous years → barred.
- Unaccepted draft: 3 years after dishonor or 10 years after date, whichever first.

“If you have an old debt, check the dates. If they never demanded payment and you haven’t paid in 10 years, it’s dead.”

🔄 UCC §3-117 – Novation: Modify Any Instrument by Separate Agreement

“The obligation of a party to pay an instrument may be modified, supplemented, or nullified by a separate agreement of the obligor and a person entitled to enforce the instrument.”

“You can change ANY contract. Send a Novation. Redefine the due date. Change the payee. Cross out the old terms and write new ones. Then sign it. The other party must accept, reject, or negotiate. Silence = acquiescence.”
