📖 UCC Article 3 - Negotiable Instruments

UCC 3-104: Definition of Negotiable Instrument
Three essential elements:
1. Unconditional promise or order to pay fixed amount
2. Payable to bearer or to order at issuance
3. No other undertaking besides payment of money
Result: Bills, invoices, statements that meet these criteria ARE negotiable instruments.
UCC 3-603: Tender of Payment
"(b) If tender of payment of an obligation to pay an instrument is made to a person entitled to enforce the instrument and the tender is refused, there is discharge, to the extent of the amount of the tender..."
Key: Proper tender + refusal = Discharge of obligation.
UCC 3-502: Dishonor Requirements
Specific procedures must be followed to dishonor an instrument. Simple refusal or return without proper notice = FAILURE to dishonor = Acceptance by operation of law.
UCC 3-109: Payable to Bearer
"If an instrument does not state a payee, it is payable to bearer."
Application: Most bills/invoices don't specify payee → automatically bearer instruments.
UCC 3-204: Endorsement
Defines special, blank, and qualified endorsements. "Without recourse" endorsement limits liability. Proper endorsement transfers rights in the instrument.

🛡️ Creditor Defenses & Counter-Strategies

❌ "We Don't Accept This"
Response: "Under UCC 3-603(b), refusal of proper tender constitutes discharge. Which UCC provision authorizes you to refuse legal tender?"
📄 "It's Just an Invoice"
Response: "Does it contain an unconditional order to pay a fixed amount? If yes, it meets UCC 3-104 definition of negotiable instrument."
⚖️ "We'll Send to Collections"
Response: "Collection attempts on a discharged debt under UCC 3-603(b) may violate FDCPA. I have properly tendered payment."
💼 "Our Policy Doesn't Allow This"
Response: "Company policy cannot override state law (UCC). The Uniform Commercial Code governs negotiable instruments, not internal policies."

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does this work for all types of debt?
A: Any obligation that produces a written demand/statement/bill that meets UCC 3-104 criteria. This includes 95% of recurring obligations.
Q: What if they refuse and sue me?
A: Perfect. You now have a UCC 3-603(b) defense: "Tender was made and refused, creating discharge." Document everything.
Q: Can I do this electronically?
A: Yes. UCC Article 3 applies to electronic records that meet the definition. Endorsement can be digital.
Q: What about federal obligations?
A: UCC is state law but federal courts apply it. Some federal obligations have specific rules - research is needed.
