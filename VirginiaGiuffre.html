A Fierce Warrior

Who Virginia Giuffre was

Virginia Giuffre was one of Jeffrey Epstein's most prominent accusers, whose testimony helped expose his abuse and relationships with powerful associates. She was recruited by Ghislaine Maxwell at age 16 and subsequently sexually abused and trafficked by Epstein.

Born Virginia Roberts in 1984, she grew up in Florida. At age 16, while working at Mar-a-Lago, she was approached by Ghislaine Maxwell and recruited into Epstein's orbit. What followed was years of abuse and trafficking to powerful men, including Prince Andrew. After escaping, she married and moved to Australia, where she raised three children and became a global advocate for survivors of sex trafficking.

⚠️ The Circumstances

Virginia Giuffre died in Western Australia on April 25, 2025, aged 41. Her family said she took her own life. Police found no evidence of foul play [citation:5]. Her family stated: "She lost her life to suicide, after being a lifelong victim of sexual abuse and sex trafficking. Virginia was a fierce warrior in the fight against sexual abuse and sex trafficking. She was the light that lifted so many survivors. In the end, the toll of abuse is so heavy that it became unbearable for Virginia to handle its weight." [citation:6][citation:12]

Her Most Important Work

The legal battles and advocacy that changed history

👑 The Prince Andrew Lawsuit

In 2021, Virginia filed a civil lawsuit against Prince Andrew, alleging she was trafficked to have sex with him on three occasions when she was 17, a minor under US law. The lawsuit led to a settlement in February 2022, with Prince Andrew making a substantial donation to Virginia's charity for sex trafficking victims [citation:9].

💰 The Settlement

Prince Andrew reportedly paid £12 million ($17 million) to settle the case, funded by loans from the royal family including Queen Elizabeth II, Prince Philip's estate, and King Charles. As of February 2026, reports indicated Andrew had "not paid back a penny" [citation:2].

📢 Speaking Out

Virginia came forward publicly after Epstein's 2009 release, becoming one of the first and most prominent accusers to speak openly. She testified at Ghislaine Maxwell's sentencing in 2022, telling the court: "I will never give up. I will never go away. If you ever get out of prison, I will be here, watching you, making sure you never hurt anyone else again." [citation:14]

🏛️ SOAR Foundation

Virginia founded Speak Out, Act, Reclaim (SOAR), a charity dedicated to "providing a safe and empowering space for survivors of sex trafficking to reclaim their stories and stand up for themselves and each other." She planned to use the settlement funds to help her organization aid sex-trafficking victims [citation:1][citation:14].

"Together, you did unthinkable things that still have a corrosive impact on me to this day. Ghislaine, the pain you have caused me is almost indescribable. Because of your choices and the world you brought me into, I don't sleep. Nightmares wake me at all hours."

— Virginia Giuffre's victim statement at Ghislaine Maxwell's sentencing, 2022 [citation:14]

Prince Andrew

The allegations and their consequences

Virginia Giuffre's allegations against Prince Andrew became the catalyst for his downfall, leading to the loss of his royal titles and a multi-million dollar settlement.

🚨 The Allegations

Virginia alleged she was trafficked by Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell to have sex with Prince Andrew on three occasions: in London in 2001 (when she was 17), at Epstein's New York mansion, and in the US Virgin Islands. She described meeting the prince at Tramp nightclub, where he was "the most hideous dancer I've ever seen in my life" and "his sweat was like it was raining basically everywhere." She said: "I knew I had to keep him happy, because it's what Jeffrey and Ghislaine would expect from me." [citation:11][citation:19]

📸 The Infamous Photo

A photograph of Prince Andrew with his arm around Virginia's bare midriff at Ghislaine Maxwell's London home became central to the case. Virginia said she gave the original to the FBI in 2011. Prince Andrew questioned whether it was his own hand in the image [citation:19].

🎙️ The BBC Interview

Prince Andrew's 2019 BBC Newsnight interview was widely criticized. He denied the allegations, said he had no recollection of meeting Virginia, and claimed he had a medical condition that meant he did not sweat. Virginia responded: "He knows what happened. I know what happened and there's only one of us telling the truth, and I know that's me." [citation:11][citation:19]

⚖️ The Settlement

In February 2022, Prince Andrew settled Virginia's civil lawsuit for a reported £12 million ($17 million). The settlement included a "substantial donation" to Virginia's charity for sex trafficking victims. Andrew did not admit guilt but acknowledged Virginia "has suffered both as an established victim of abuse and as a result of unfair public attacks" and "regrets his association with Epstein." [citation:9]

👑 Loss of Titles

Following the scandal, Prince Andrew was stripped of his honorary military titles and royal patronages. In October 2025, following the publication of Virginia's memoir, he renounced his titles and membership in the Order of the Garter [citation:16].

"Nobody's Girl"

Virginia's posthumous memoir

Virginia's memoir, "Nobody's Girl: A Memoir of Surviving Abuse and Fighting for Justice," was published posthumously in October 2025 and topped Amazon's bestseller lists upon release [citation:1].

👑 The Prince Andrew Account

Virginia described three occasions when she was trafficked to have sex with Prince Andrew, including twice when she was only 17. She recalled that when the prince correctly guessed her age, he noted: "My daughters are just a little younger than you." She wrote that Epstein paid her $15,000 for her night with the prince [citation:1].

🏌️ Donald Trump

Virginia described being introduced to Trump by her father at Mar-a-Lago in 2000, when she was 16. She said Trump "couldn't have been friendlier" and offered her work as a babysitter for his friends. It was at Mar-a-Lago that she was noticed and groomed by Ghislaine Maxwell [citation:1].

❓ The Mystery Politician

Virginia described being "brutally raped" by a politician she did not name. In the UK version of the memoir, she called him a "former minister"; in the US version, a "prime minister." No reason was given for the discrepancy [citation:1].

💔 The Miscarriage

In summer 2001, Virginia was admitted to a New York emergency room after participating in an orgy with Epstein, Andrew, and eight other girls. Epstein lied about her age. She was informed she had suffered a miscarriage. She noted that Epstein "never wore a condom," and neither did the men he and Maxwell "trafficked me to." [citation:1]

👻 Haunted by "Hungry Ghosts"

Virginia wrote that she was still haunted by the "hungry ghosts" of Epstein and Maxwell decades after escaping their "house of shame." She wrote: "Still I feel haunted by their hungry ghosts." [citation:16]

🔒 Protecting Her Children

Virginia wrote that the main reason she did not name some alleged abusers in the memoir was to protect her three children: "Maybe in the future I will be ready to talk about these men. But not now." [citation:1]

⚠️ Critical Note

Virginia had stated she was a "fierce warrior" and had dedicated her life to fighting for survivors. Her family said: "It was when she held her newborn daughter in her arms that Virginia realized she had to fight back against those who had abused her and so many others." Despite her advocacy, the toll of her abuse ultimately became unbearable. She is survived by her three children: Christian, Noah, and Emily [citation:8].
