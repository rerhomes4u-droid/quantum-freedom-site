A Voice for the Voiceless

Who Jesús Ramiro Zapata was

Jesús Ramiro Zapata was a Colombian teacher, trade unionist, and human rights defender who dedicated his life to exposing the truth about massacres and state-paramilitary collusion in Segovia, Antioquia. He was a co-founder of the Comité de Derechos Humanos de Segovia and a member of the Colectivo de Derechos Humanos Semillas de Libertad (CODEHSEL) [citation:10][citation:15].

Born on January 20, 1952, Zapata worked as a teacher affiliated with the Secretary of Education and Culture of Antioquia. He was appointed Defender of Human Rights by the Municipal Council of Segovia and worked tirelessly to bring justice for the victims of the 1988 and 1996 massacres in Segovia and Remedios [citation:6][citation:15].

⚠️ The Circumstances

On May 3, 2000, Jesús Ramiro Zapata was kidnapped from a billiard hall in Segovia by two armed men who identified themselves as members of the United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC). He was forced into a taxi and taken to a location called "Marmajito" (also referred to as "Charco de las Brujas"), where he was executed with multiple gunshots to the head [citation:2][citation:12]. He was 48 years old. Despite having precautionary measures granted by the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights in 1998, the Colombian State failed to protect him [citation:3][citation:11].

His Life's Mission

Exposing the truth in Segovia

📋 Co-Founder of Human Rights Committee

Zapata co-founded the Comité de Derechos Humanos de Segovia and was a member of CODEHSEL (Colectivo de Derechos Humanos Semillas de Libertad). These organizations documented human rights violations and provided support to victims of the armed conflict [citation:10][citation:15].

Organization

🔍 Investigating Massacres

He worked to clarify the truth about the 1988, 1996, and 1997 massacres in Segovia. The 1988 massacre alone resulted in 43 deaths and 50 injured, with the State later acknowledging its international responsibility for the paramilitary actions carried out with State acquiescence [citation:15].

Truth-Seeking

⚖️ Denouncing State-Paramilitary Collusion

Zapata publicly denounced the collaboration between State security forces and paramilitary groups in the massacres and assassinations of union members. This work made him a target of State and parastatal persecution [citation:6][citation:15].

Dangerous Truth

👨‍🏫 Teacher and Unionist

As a teacher affiliated with the Secretary of Education of Antioquia, Zapata worked in various educational institutions. He was also a trade unionist, advocating for teachers' rights and social justice [citation:15].

Education

🛡️ Appointed Defender of Human Rights

The Municipal Council of Segovia officially designated Zapata as a Defender of Human Rights, recognizing his work and commitment to justice for the community [citation:15].

Recognition

💔 Surviving Persecution

Between 1994 and 1997, Zapata was subjected to illegal intelligence activities, false accusations of being an ELN member, arbitrary detentions, home raids, and phone interceptions. He was forced to displace to Medellín in 1997 but had to return to Segovia in 1998 due to economic hardship [citation:2][citation:15].

Survival

"The case of the social leader reached the Inter-American Court after the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights concluded that Colombia is responsible for his death."

— El Espectador [citation:6]

The Quest for Justice

26 years of impunity and the Inter-American Court ruling

🚨 The Assassination

On May 3, 2000, at approximately 10:30 PM, Zapata was playing cards at the "Monterrey" billiard hall in Segovia when two armed men entered and identified themselves as members of the United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC). They forced him into a taxi and took him to "Marmajito," where he was executed with multiple gunshots. His nephew, Adrián Alberto Zapata, located the taxi driver who revealed the location. When the family went to the police, they were told to "go pick up the body themselves" as the prosecutor would do the official recovery in the morning [citation:2][citation:12].

📅 Precautionary Measures Ignored

On February 11, 1998, the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR) granted precautionary measures in favor of Zapata, recognizing the grave risk he faced. Despite this, the Colombian State failed to guarantee his safety. The last official communication about his security situation was on May 12, 1998 [citation:3][citation:19].

State Failure

🔍 Illegal Intelligence and Criminalization

Between 1994 and 1997, Zapata was falsely identified as a member of the ELN guerrilla group in military intelligence reports. This led to five unfounded criminal investigations against him, a home raid in May 1996 where evidence was allegedly "planted," phone interceptions, and arbitrary detentions [citation:2][citation:6][citation:15].

Criminalization

🏛️ Inter-American Court Ruling (2026)

On February 23, 2026, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights declared Colombia internationally responsible for violations of Zapata's rights to life, personal integrity, personal liberty, honor and dignity, work, judicial guarantees, judicial protection, and the autonomous right to defend human rights [citation:3][citation:4].

International Justice

⚖️ State Acknowledgment of Responsibility

On March 9, 2026, the Colombian State, through the ANDJE, officially recognized its international responsibility for the violations against Zapata. The State admitted that Zapata was subjected to illegal intelligence activities and that police forces contributed to a risk situation that forced his displacement [citation:1][citation:17].

Accountability

📜 Reparations Ordered

The Court ordered the State to: continue and conclude criminal investigations, rectify intelligence archives, hold a public act of acknowledgment of responsibility, install a memorial plaque at the school where Zapata worked, produce a documentary about his life and work, and provide medical and psychological treatment to his family [citation:1][citation:9][citation:17].

Reparations

❌ 26 Years of Impunity

The criminal investigations into Zapata's murder were characterized by irregularities, unjustified delays, and a lack of procedural progress. No one has been identified, prosecuted, or punished for the material or intellectual authors of the crime. The Court emphasized that this impunity has perpetuated the suffering of his family and community [citation:3][citation:10].

Impunity

"The Court determined that the threats, judicial harassment and acts of persecution against Mr. Zapata were a form of intimidation, which greatly affected his mental integrity and were directly related to his work as a human rights defender."

— Inter-American Court of Human Rights [citation:3]

⚠️ Critical Note

Days before his assassination, Zapata had denounced to local authorities that paramilitaries were asking about him: "From April 15 to Friday the 28th, some new paramilitaries who don't know me have asked about me 8 times." Despite this warning and the precautionary measures from the IACHR, the State failed to protect him [citation:11].
