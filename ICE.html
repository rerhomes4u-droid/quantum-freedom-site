▣ KNOW YOUR RIGHTS
Stay calm. Do not run. Do not lie. Do not open the door without a judge-signed warrant.

▣ THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

An ICE administrative warrant is not a judicial warrant.

"Slide it under the door."

If it is not signed by a judge, you do not have to open the door.

🚪 ICE IS AT YOUR DOOR

STEP 1
Do not open the door. Opening the door does not give them permission to enter, but it is safer to speak through the door.
STEP 2
Ask: "Are you immigration agents? What are you here for?" Ask them to show a badge or ID through the window or peephole.
STEP 3
Ask: "Do you have a warrant signed by a judge?" If they say yes, ask them to slide it under the door or hold it up to the window.
CRITICAL
An ICE administrative warrant (Form I-200, I-205) is NOT a judicial warrant. It does not give them the right to enter your home. Only a warrant signed by a judge does.
⚠ In 2025, ICE issued an internal memo claiming agents may force entry with only an administrative warrant. Legal advocates say this violates the Fourth Amendment. The ACLU's guidance remains: do not open the door without a judge-signed warrant. If they force entry, do not resist.
"I do not consent to your entry. I am exercising my right to remain silent. I wish to speak with a lawyer."
DO NOT: Open the door. Sign anything. Lie. Resist if they force entry. Say anything about your status or birthplace.
IF THEY FORCE ENTRY
Do not resist. State clearly: "I do not consent to this entry or search. I am exercising my right to remain silent. I want a lawyer." Then stay silent.

🚶 ICE STOPPED YOU OUTSIDE

STEP 1
Stay calm. Do not run. Do not argue. Keep your hands visible.
STEP 2
Ask: "Am I free to go?"
IF YES
You can calmly and silently walk away. Do not answer questions.
IF NO
You are being detained. Say: "I want to remain silent. I want to speak to a lawyer."
DO NOT
Answer questions about where you were born, your citizenship, or how you entered the country. Do not consent to a search.
"I do not consent to a search. I am exercising my right to remain silent."

🚗 ICE PULLED YOU OVER

STEP 1
Pull over safely. Turn off the engine. Turn on the interior light. Keep your hands on the wheel.
STEP 2
Show your driver's license, registration, and proof of insurance if requested.
STEP 3
You do not have to answer questions about your immigration status. Say: "I remain silent."
DO NOT: Consent to a search of your car. Do not lie. Do not provide false documents.

🏢 ICE IS AT YOUR WORK

PUBLIC AREA
If you are in a public area (lobby, parking lot, dining area), ICE may approach you. You still have the right to remain silent.
PRIVATE AREA
If ICE wants to enter a private area (kitchen, office, storage), they need either your consent or a judicial warrant. You can say no.
DOCUMENTS
ICE may have a subpoena for records. A subpoena is not a warrant. You have the right to contest it in court.
"I do not consent to a search of this area. Please show me a judicial warrant."

🪪 POLICE ASKED ABOUT MY STATUS

YOUR RIGHT
You have the right to remain silent. You do not have to discuss your immigration or citizenship status with police, immigration agents, or other officials. Anything you tell an officer can later be used against you in immigration court.
IF DRIVING
The officer can require you to show your license, vehicle registration, and proof of insurance. You do not have to answer questions about your immigration status.
IF YOU ARE A GREEN CARD HOLDER
If you are a lawful permanent resident who has maintained your status, you only have to answer questions establishing your identity and permanent residency. Refusal to answer other questions may cause delay, but officials may not deny you entry into the United States for failure to answer other questions.
IF YOU ARE ON A VISA
If you are a non-citizen visa holder, you may be denied entry into the U.S. if you refuse to answer officers' questions.
DO NOT: Lie about your status or provide false documents. If you are over 18 and have immigration documents, carry them with you.

🌵 I'M NEAR THE BORDER

THE 100-MILE ZONE
Border Patrol claims authority within 100 air miles of any external boundary of the U.S. — including the entire coastline. Nearly two-thirds of the U.S. population lives in this zone. Most of the 10 largest cities fall in it. Florida lies entirely within it.
AT CHECKPOINTS
At immigration checkpoints, agents do not need any suspicion to stop you and ask you questions. But their questions should be brief and related to verifying immigration status. They can also visually inspect your vehicle.
YOUR RIGHT
You have the right to remain silent. You can also tell the agent that you will only answer questions in the presence of an attorney, no matter your citizenship or immigration status.
EXPEDITED REMOVAL
People who entered the U.S. without inspection by an immigration official may be subject to expedited removal. If you are told you are subject to expedited removal, ask for the stated reason. If you fear persecution if returned to your country of origin, immediately inform the agents of your fear.
AT BORDER CROSSINGS
At border crossings, federal authorities do not need a warrant or even suspicion of wrongdoing to justify a "routine search," such as searching luggage or a vehicle.
DO NOT: Flee from an immigration checkpoint. Stay calm. Do not lie or provide false documents.

🚌 I WAS STOPPED ON A BUS/TRAIN

WHERE THIS HAPPENS
Border Patrol agents may board buses and trains in the 100-mile border region either at the station or while the bus is on its journey. More than one officer usually boards.
WHAT THEY ASK
They will ask passengers questions about their immigration status, ask passengers to show them immigration documents, or both. These questions should be brief and related to verifying one's lawful presence in the U.S.
YOUR RIGHT
You are not required to answer and can simply say you do not wish to do so. You have the right to remain silent.
"I do not wish to answer questions."
IF YOU ARE ON AN AIRPLANE
A pilot may refuse to fly a passenger if he or she reasonably believes the passenger is a threat to flight safety. A pilot may not, however, question you or refuse to allow you on a flight because of bias based on your religion, race, national origin, gender, ethnicity, or political beliefs.

🔒 YOU ARE DETAINED

STEP 1
Say clearly: "I want to remain silent. I want to speak to a lawyer."
STEP 2
Do not sign anything. Do not answer questions about your status. Do not sign a "Stipulated Removal Order" or agree to "voluntary departure" without a lawyer.
STEP 3
Ask for a list of free or low-cost lawyers. The government does not have to provide one, but you have the right to hire one and to have one present at hearings.
YOUR RIGHTS
You have the right to contact your consulate. You have the right to a bond hearing before an immigration judge. You have the right to have your attorney with you at any hearing.
REMEMBER: Your 'A' number. Give it to your family. They can use ICE's detainee locator to find you. Keep a copy of your immigration documents with someone you trust.

🛠 SPECIAL TOOLS & LINKS

JUDICIAL WARRANT
SIGNED BY A JUDGE
You MUST comply
ICE ADMINISTRATIVE WARRANT
SIGNED BY ICE
You do NOT have to comply
LOOK FOR
A judge's signature. A court seal. The words 'United States District Court.' If it says 'Department of Homeland Security' or 'ICE,' it is NOT a judicial warrant.
