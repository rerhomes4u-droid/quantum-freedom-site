THE PHYSIOLOGY OF FEAR

A mask restricts airflow. The virus is 0.125 microns. The pores in a surgical mask are between 30 and 80 microns. The virus passes through the mask as easily as a mosquito passes through a chain-link fence. But carbon dioxide does not.

When you wear a mask for hours, you are re-breathing your own expelled carbon dioxide. This causes hypercapnia—an increase in carbon dioxide in the bloodstream. The symptoms are headaches, fatigue, dizziness, and a feeling of suffocation.

They told you that the feeling of suffocation was a sign that the mask was "working." They told you that you were "saving lives."

THE CULT OF THE MASK

In his book The Crowd: A Study of the Popular Mind, Gustave Le Bon wrote that the primary characteristic of a crowd is the lowering of the individual's intelligence. The individual becomes suggestible, emotional, and willing to sacrifice their own interests for the illusion of belonging to a group.

The mask was the ritualistic symbol of the new global religion.

Think about what the mask did to human connection. It covered the mouth. It covered the nose. It hid the micro-expressions that humans use to determine if another person is safe, trustworthy, or a threat.

60 to 65 percent of human communication is non-verbal. The lower half of the face is the most expressive part of the human body. It is where we smile, where we frown, where we express disgust, surprise, and joy.

When you force an entire population to cover the bottom half of their face, you are not protecting them from a virus. You are dehumanizing them.

THE CHILDREN (THE UNFORGIVABLE SIN)

If the mask was psychologically damaging for adults, what did it do to children?

Infants and toddlers learn to read faces before they learn to speak. They learn empathy by watching their parents smile. They learn emotional regulation by mirroring the expressions of their caregivers.

Imagine a child who spends the first three years of their life in a world where every face they see is covered by a piece of cloth. They can't see the smile. They can't see the frown. They can't see the grimace of confusion.

They see eyes. And eyes, without the context of a mouth, are ambiguous. They can look happy, or they can look scared. The child's nervous system is constantly on edge, trying to interpret the uninterpretable.

The psychological trauma of the masked child is incalculable. We have raised a generation of children who were denied the basic human right of seeing a face. We told them that to be safe, they had to be muzzled.

They broke your spirit with a piece of cloth. And they did it without firing a single shot.
