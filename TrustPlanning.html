Trust Planning: Protecting Your Loved Ones

No one likes to think about what happens when they're gone. But it's important to plan for the future and protect your loved ones.

What is a Trust?

A trust is a powerful tool for managing your assets and ensuring they are passed down to your heirs according to your wishes. With a trust, you can avoid the time-consuming and expensive probate process and maintain more control over your assets, even after you're gone.

Benefits of Trusts

  • Avoid Probate: Bypass the lengthy and costly court-supervised process of distributing assets.
  • Maintain Control: Specify exactly how and when your assets should be distributed to beneficiaries.
  • Protection: Shield assets from potential creditors, lawsuits, and other legal claims.
  • Tax Efficiency: Minimize gift and estate tax liabilities through strategic planning.
  • Customization: Tailor the trust to meet your specific needs and goals, whether for education, charity, or special needs care.
  • Privacy: Unlike wills, trusts are private documents that don't become public record.

Key Trust Terminology

Grantor/Settlor: The person who creates the trust.

Trustee: The person or institution that manages the trust assets.

Beneficiary: The person(s) who benefit from the trust.

Revocable Trust: Can be changed or terminated by the grantor during their lifetime.

Irrevocable Trust: Generally cannot be changed once established, offering greater asset protection.
