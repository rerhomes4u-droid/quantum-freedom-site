A Life for the Children

Who Kenneth Akena Watmon was

Kenneth Akena Watmon was a Ugandan child rights activist and Community Development Officer who dedicated his life to protecting vulnerable children in Northern Uganda. He worked with ACODEV, a non-governmental organization in Kasese district, and previously served as a Community Development Officer in his native Pader district[citation:1].

As a child, Kenneth survived the brutality of the Lord's Resistance Army (LRA) insurgency. In 2003, while a student at St Mary's Seminary Lacor in Gulu, LRA rebels attacked his dormitory and abducted about 40 of his colleagues. Kenneth survived by hiding underneath a bed[citation:9]. That trauma ignited in him a lifelong passion for protecting children from suffering.

⚠️ The Circumstances

On November 12, 2016, Kenneth was shot in the stomach at Forest Mall in Lugogo, Kampala, after he reportedly scratched a businessman's car while reversing. He had stepped out of his vehicle to apologize when he was shot at close range[citation:1][citation:15]. He died hours later at Norvic Hospital. He was 33 years old, just two weeks before his 34th birthday, and was engaged to be married the following February[citation:9].

The Quest for Justice

The trial and traditional reconciliation

🚨 The Killing

On November 12, 2016, Kenneth scratched a car belonging to businessman Mathew Kanyamunyu while reversing at Forest Mall in Lugogo, Kampala. He stepped out of his vehicle to apologize. According to witnesses and Kenneth's own dying declaration, Kanyamunyu lowered his window and shot Kenneth in the stomach at close range[citation:1][citation:15]. Kenneth died hours later at Norvic Hospital[citation:2].

🔫 The Missing Gun

Police never recovered the murder weapon or the bullet cartridge from the scene. A bullet was recovered from Kenneth's body during postmortem, but the killer gun remains missing. This lack of forensic evidence complicated the prosecution's case[citation:20].

Evidence Gap

⚖️ Murder Charges Reduced

Mathew Kanyamunyu was initially charged with murder alongside his girlfriend Cynthia Munwangari and his brother Joseph. The charges against the girlfriend were later dropped. Kanyamunyu's murder charge was reduced to manslaughter through a plea bargain[citation:3][citation:10].

Plea Bargain

🤝 Mato Oput Ceremony

Before his criminal trial concluded, Kanyamunyu sought reconciliation through "Mato Oput," a traditional Acholi justice mechanism. He confessed to killing Kenneth before Acholi elders and agreed to pay 10 cows and 3 goats as reparation to the family[citation:7][citation:19].

Traditional Justice

💔 Family Forgives

Kenneth's family accepted Kanyamunyu's apology and forgave him through the Mato Oput ceremony, though they continued to pursue criminal justice through the courts[citation:13].

Reconciliation

🏛️ Five-Year Sentence

On November 12, 2020—the fourth anniversary of Kenneth's death—Justice Stephen Mubiru sentenced Kanyamunyu to five years in prison for manslaughter. The judge noted Kanyamunyu was a first-time offender who rushed Kenneth to the hospital[citation:3].

Conviction

🚪 Early Release

Kanyamunyu was released from Luzira Prison in April 2024 after serving three years and five months, receiving remission for good behavior. He was originally sentenced to five years and one month[citation:10][citation:18].

Released

"The stupidity, the foolishness, the evil that I exhibited on that day is not something I knew was in me. But now I recognise, deep within me, there is evil that I did not know about."

— Mathew Kanyamunyu, during Mato Oput ceremony[citation:19]

⚠️ The Family's Continued Pain

As of November 2025—three years after Kenneth's death—his family was still waiting for closure. His brother Jordan Yhang told Uganda Radio Network: "Our hope is going further down and right now we are only praying because family has exhausted all the options available but nothing is yielding"[citation:1].

The Akena Foundation

Preserving his legacy for the children

In November 2017, during the first annual memorial service for Kenneth, his family launched the Akena Foundation to honor his memory and continue his work. The foundation was conceived "from their painful loss and the need to preserve Akena's memory and legacy"[citation:8].

🎯 Mission

The Akena Foundation focuses on "fighting for justice and supporting vulnerable children access social justice and other services that will help better their lives"[citation:8].

Child Advocacy

🕊️ Priority: Gun Violence Orphans

The foundation's first priority is children who have been orphaned due to gun-related violence—a direct response to Kenneth's own death by gun violence[citation:8].

Legacy

📚 Education Support

The foundation has already initiated activities including paying school fees for children in Kitgum, including at Uganda Martyrs Centenary Primary School[citation:8].

Education

💰 Community Funding

Over 10 million Ugandan shillings in cash and pledges was raised to kick-start the foundation. Membership contribution cards were designed at Silver (50,000 UGX), Gold (100,000 UGX), and Platinum (200,000 UGX) yearly levels[citation:8].

Sustainable

🕯️ Annual Memorial

Kenneth's family and friends hold annual memorial services. The third annual service was held at Christ the King Church in Kitgum Municipality[citation:1].

Remembrance

👥 Family Leadership

Kenneth's brother Jordan Ocakacon explained the foundation was conceived from their painful loss, and his eldest brother Jordan Yhang has been a vocal advocate for justice[citation:1][citation:8].

Family
