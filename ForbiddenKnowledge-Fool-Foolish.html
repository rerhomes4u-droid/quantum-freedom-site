FOOL vs. FOOLISH

The Willingness Test
for Ramiro · from his father · via Santiago Ávila

My father sent me a message one day about the difference between a fool and a foolish man. I read it three times and then I sat with it. The distinction is not a joke. It is a scalpel.

The one who does not know

FOOL

Wrong because he does not know. We are all fools about something. Recoverable: he thanks you for helping, he changes when he learns. Being a fool is temporary.

The one who will not know

FOOLISH

He knows. He has the data. He sees the evidence. And he denies it. He is offended if corrected, he affirms his error harder when everything goes wrong. Being foolish is optional.

This app does not tell you that you are a fool. It does not tell you that you are foolish. It shows you the structure of the difference — twelve markers that separate the two categories — and it asks you, honestly, to mark where you have been in the conversations that matter most. Not as an insult. As a diagnostic. Because the only way to stay out of the second category is to keep checking whether you have slipped into it.

There is no "skip." There is no "neutral." There is only fool, foolish, or neither. Answer honestly. The mirror does not lie, but it only shows what you bring to it.
