01Evidence Summary — For the Attorney

The attorney does not need the whole archive. The attorney needs the record that supports the beneficiary's claim. This is the summary the attorney receives.

The Record — What the Attorney Needs to Know

The UN has found genocide. The UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry found that Israel continues to commit genocide in Gaza despite the October 2025 ceasefire. The report documents "extermination, persecution, torture, rape and other forms of sexual violence" [citation:2].

Children are targeted. The UN found that Israeli forces have "deliberately targeted Palestinian children." Save the Children documents "severe physical, psychological and sexual abuse of Palestinian children in Israeli military detention, including reports of torture and other ill-treatment" [citation:7].

Sexual violence is documented. The UN Special Rapporteur on torture counted 33 incidents of sexual torture and other sexualised ill-treatment. Reports include "one case of repeated anal and vaginal rape, and two cases of object-rape" [citation:5].

Organ theft concerns are documented, not proven. The Palestinian Center for Missing and Disappeared Persons reported that Israel returned 54 bodies and 66 boxes of fragmented remains "without any medical reports or official documentation," raising "staggering" concerns over organ theft. The center called for an independent international investigation [citation:3]. The claim is not verified. It is documented as a concern.

The record is the basis for the refugee claim. The attorney uses the UN findings, the Save the Children report, and the Special Rapporteur's report to support the claim of well-founded fear of persecution. The beneficiary's own testimony is the primary evidence. The UN reports are the corroboration.

03Host Readiness Checklist — For the Sanctuary Coordinator

The host is the most vulnerable point in the chain. This is the checklist the Coordinator completes before the host is approved.

Host Readiness — Before the First Arrival
  • PMA membership in good standing for 90+ days.
  • Background check completed. Criminal record check in Colombia and country of residence.
  • Home inspection completed. The room is safe, clean, lockable. Utilities working.
  • Interview completed. The Coordinator has interviewed the host. Motivation, expectations, capacity.
  • Two references from PMA members. Verified.
  • Host agreement signed.
  • Insurance confirmed. Homeowner's or renter's insurance covers guests.
  • Registered with the Colombian attorney.
  • Stipend bank account set up.
  • 24/7 emergency contact provided.
  • House rules agreed. Smoking, alcohol, visitors, noise, pets.
  • Duration agreed. Initial period: ___ months, starting ______, ending ______.
  • Backup host identified. In case the primary host is compromised.

06What the Field Manual Does Not Do

The field manual does not replace the attorney. The attorney reviews every intake, every document, every legal path. The field manual is the preparation. The attorney is the authority.
The field manual does not publish the rescued. The names, the locations, the routes, the hosts, the code words are never public. The field manual is confidential. The public sees the ledger. The public does not see the people.
The field manual does not promise success. The extraction may fail. The visa may be denied. The host may be compromised. The field manual prepares for the failure as much as the success. The failure protocol is in the Missing Fifteen.
