⚖️ Advanced Courtroom Questioning Strategy

For Carolina Herrera - Master the art of non-conceding questions that force the agency to prove its case without giving up any ground

Core Principles of Non-Conceding Questions:

Never assume their premise: Questions that start with "why do you claim..." already concede they have a valid claim.
Demand definitions first: Before addressing any allegation, require precise definitions of all terms.
Require citation, not argument: Your words become their evidence. Make them cite specific laws and regulations.
Force them to state their position: Never explain your position until they've fully stated theirs with supporting authority.
Question order matters: Start with jurisdiction, then definitions, then evidence, then procedure.

Master Question Library

Each question is designed to force the agency to prove its case without conceding any ground. Study the differences between "bad" (conceding) and "good" (non-conceding) questions.

📍 Jurisdiction & Authority Questions
Conceding
"Why do you have jurisdiction over me?"
❌ Problem:
Assumes jurisdiction exists and merely questions "why." You've already accepted their authority by asking this way.
Non-Conceding
"Before we proceed, please state for the record: What is the specific jurisdictional basis for this proceeding, and where is that jurisdiction established in law?"
✅ Why this works:
Doesn't assume jurisdiction exists. Forces them to cite specific legal authority. Puts burden on them to establish jurisdiction as a prerequisite.
Non-Conceding
"Please provide the specific statute, regulation, or constitutional provision that grants you authority over my fundamental parental rights."
✅ Why this works:
Demands citation, not argument. Focuses on "fundamental parental rights" which require explicit authority to interfere with.
Non-Conceding
"Where in the record is there evidence of my consent to this court's jurisdiction over my relationship with my child?"
✅ Why this works:
Attacks jurisdiction at its weakest point - lack of consent. Makes them prove you agreed to their authority.
📄 Evidence & Allegation Questions
Conceding
"Why are you making these false allegations against me?"
❌ Problem:
Uses emotional language ("false allegations") and concedes there are actual "allegations" to defend against.
Non-Conceding
"Please identify for the record: What specific evidence supports the claims that have been presented, and where is that evidence documented in the official record?"
✅ Why this works:
Uses neutral language ("claims" not "allegations"). Forces them to point to actual evidence. If they can't, it exposes the lack of substance.
Non-Conceding
"Can you please define for the record what you mean by 'endangerment' and cite the legal definition you are applying in this case?"
✅ Why this works:
Attacks vague terms first. Makes them define their terms before you respond. Often they use emotional terms without legal definitions.
Non-Conceding
"Where is the sworn testimony or verified evidence that establishes the factual basis for the actions taken against my parental rights?"
✅ Why this works:
Demands verified evidence, not just claims. "Sworn testimony" requires under-oath statements they likely don't have.
⚖️ Procedural & Due Process Questions
Conceding
"Why wasn't I given a hearing before my child was taken?"
❌ Problem:
Assumes they had authority to take the child and merely questions the timing of the hearing.
Non-Conceding
"Please identify the specific emergency or exigent circumstances that justified bypassing normal due process protections, and where is that justification documented?"
✅ Why this works:
Recognizes that due process can only be bypassed for emergencies. Forces them to prove an emergency existed (which they can't after 2 months).
Non-Conceding
"Can you please point to where in the record I was provided notice of these proceedings and an opportunity to be heard before any action was taken affecting my fundamental rights?"
✅ Why this works:
Focuses on specific due process requirements (notice + opportunity to be heard). Makes them prove compliance with constitutional requirements.
Non-Conceding
"What procedural rules or statutes authorize continuing this separation for [X] days without a full adversarial hearing with the right to cross-examine witnesses?"
✅ Why this works:
Highlights the unconstitutional delay. Makes them cite authority for prolonged deprivation without hearing.
📝 Contract & Consent Questions
Conceding
"I never signed a contract with you!"
❌ Problem:
Statement, not a question. Emotional. Doesn't force them to prove contract exists.
Non-Conceding
"Please produce for the record the written agreement or contract wherein I consented to the terms you are attempting to enforce, showing my signature and the consideration exchanged."
✅ Why this works:
Demands specific contractual elements (signature, consideration). Most agency actions lack actual contracts.
Non-Conceding
"Where is the documentation showing I waived my fundamental parental rights, and what specific rights was I made aware I was waiving?"
✅ Why this works:
Waiver of fundamental rights requires explicit, knowing consent. Forces them to prove you knowingly waived specific rights.
Non-Conceding
"Can you please identify the specific benefit or consideration I received in exchange for submitting to this agency's authority over my family relationships?"
✅ Why this works:
Highlights lack of mutual agreement. Contracts require consideration (something given in exchange).
🛡️ Oath of Office & Surety Bond Questions
Conceding
"You don't have the right to be a judge!"
❌ Problem:
Emotional statement, not a question. Confrontational without legal basis.
Non-Conceding
"For the record, please provide the location where your Oath of Office is filed with the circuit clerk, and the Surety Bond number that guarantees my remedy if injured in these proceedings."
✅ Why this works:
Professional, procedural question. Judges MUST have filed oaths and bonds. If they can't produce, they lack proper authority.
Non-Conceding
"Can you please direct me to where in the public records your commission, oath, and bond are recorded, so I may verify the court's authority to proceed?"
✅ Why this works:
Frames it as your right to verify authority. Makes refusal to provide look like hiding something.
Non-Conceding
"What is the procedure for making a claim against your Surety Bond if I am injured by unlawful actions in these proceedings?"
✅ Why this works:
Assumes injury has occurred and seeks remedy. Puts them on notice you know your rights regarding bonds.
🎯 Questioning Strategy Reminder

Your questions are NOT arguments. They are tools to:

  1. Force them to state their position with supporting authority
  2. Expose gaps in their evidence and procedure
  3. Build a record for appeal when they can't answer
  4. Maintain your position as the wronged party seeking verification

Never answer their questions until they've fully answered yours about jurisdiction, authority, and evidence.

Live Question Practice

⚠️ Critical Practice Rules:

1. NO emotional language - use precise, legal terminology

2. ALWAYS demand definitions first - "Please define what you mean by X"

3. NEVER assume their premise - question the foundation, not the conclusion

4. ALWAYS require citation - "What specific statute/regulation..."

5. MAINTAIN the burden on them - you're verifying, not defending

Practice Scenario 1: DSS Social Worker Testimony

Social Worker says: "Ms. Herrera, we had to act quickly to protect your child from the dangerous environment in your home on October 18."

❌ Bad Response (what NOT to say):
"That's a lie! My home wasn't dangerous! You're making this up!" (Emotional, concedes there's an allegation to defend against)

Expert Example Question:

"Please define for the record what you mean by 'dangerous environment,' and cite the specific standards or criteria you're applying. Then, please identify where in your investigation report you documented this alleged danger, and what specific evidence supports that finding."

✅ This works because: Demands definition first, requires citation to standards, asks for documented evidence, uses neutral language ("alleged").

Practice Scenario 2: Judge's Authority Challenge

Judge says: "Ms. Herrera, I don't need to show you my oath. We have a hearing to conduct about the DSS findings."

❌ Bad Response (what NOT to say):
"You have to show me! You're not a real judge without it!" (Confrontational, emotional, makes you look unreasonable)

Expert Example Question:

"Your Honor, with respect, jurisdiction is the first question in any legal proceeding. For the record, can you please direct me to where in the public records your Oath of Office and Surety Bond are filed, so I may verify this court's authority to proceed on matters affecting my fundamental parental rights?"

✅ This works because: Professional, cites legal principle (jurisdiction first), frames it as verification right, mentions "fundamental parental rights" requiring proper authority.

Practice Scenario 3: Attorney's Allegations

DSS Attorney says: "The evidence shows you endangered your child, which justifies our intervention under South Carolina child protection laws."

❌ Bad Response:
"What evidence? There is no evidence! Show me this so-called evidence!" (Still emotional, still reacting to their frame)

Expert Example Question:

"For the record, please identify the specific South Carolina statute you're referring to, and read the exact language that defines 'endangerment' as you're applying it. Then, please point to the specific, admissible evidence in the record that meets each element of that statutory definition."

✅ This works because: Demands exact statutory citation, requires definition from statute (not their interpretation), asks for element-by-element evidence matching.

Complete Questioning Strategy

🎯 The 4-Phase Questioning Strategy

Phase 1: Foundation Questions (Before ANY substantive discussion)

  • "Before we proceed, for the record, what is the jurisdictional basis for this proceeding?"
  • "Please identify where your authority is established in law or constitution."
  • "Where is your Oath of Office and Surety Bond filed for verification?"
  • "What specific statute grants you authority over fundamental parental rights?"

If they can't answer these, the proceeding lacks foundation. Document their failure to answer.

Phase 2: Definition Questions (Before responding to ANY allegations)

  • "Please define for the record what you mean by [danger, endangerment, risk, etc.]"
  • "What legal definition are you applying from which statute or regulation?"
  • "What are the specific elements that must be proven under that definition?"
  • "How was I made aware of these definitions and standards?"

Phase 3: Evidence Questions (Make them prove EVERY element)

  • "Please identify the specific evidence supporting each element of your claim."
  • "Where is that evidence documented in the official record?"
  • "Is this evidence sworn testimony or verified documentation?"
  • "What investigation was conducted to verify this evidence?"
  • "Where is the record of my opportunity to respond to this evidence?"

Phase 4: Procedure & Due Process Questions

  • "What specific emergency justified bypassing normal due process?"
  • "Where is that emergency documented and when was it resolved?"
  • "What procedure authorizes [X] days of separation without hearing?"
  • "Where is the record of notice provided to me?"
  • "What is the remedy if this proceeding is found unlawful?"

🔥 Advanced Techniques

1. The "Mirror" Technique

When they ask you a question, respond with a question that exposes their assumptions:

They ask: "Why did you endanger your child on October 18?"
You respond: "Before I can address that, I need you to first define what you mean by 'endanger' and identify the specific evidence you're referring to."

2. The "Burden Shifting" Question

Questions that automatically put burden back on them:

"What investigation did you conduct to verify the allegations before taking action?"

3. The "Record Building" Question

Questions designed to create appealable issues:

"For the record, please state whether you are refusing to provide verification of your authority to proceed."

4. The "Time Trap" Question

Exposes unconstitutional delays:

"What statutory authority permits [54] days of separation without a full hearing with cross-examination rights?"

⚠️ What NEVER to Do:

❌ NEVER say "I'm not liable" - instead ask "What law creates the alleged liability?"

❌ NEVER say "You have no jurisdiction" - instead ask "What is your jurisdictional basis?"

❌ NEVER say "That's a lie" - instead ask "What evidence supports that claim?"

❌ NEVER explain your position first - make them state theirs fully with evidence

❌ NEVER use emotional language - use precise legal terminology only

Optimal Question Flow & Order

Start: "For the record, I appear under duress to address the unlawful separation from my child."

📝 Script for Opening Statement with Built-in Questions

"Your Honor, for the record, I appear under duress and coercion due to the unlawful separation from my child for [54] days without a court order.

Before we can proceed to any substantive discussion, I need to establish several foundational matters for the record:

First, what is the specific jurisdictional basis for this proceeding, and where is that jurisdiction established in law?

Second, where is your Oath of Office and Surety Bond filed with the circuit clerk for verification?

Third, what specific statute grants authority over my fundamental parental rights?

Fourth, what emergency or exigent circumstances justified bypassing normal due process protections?

Fifth, where is the court order authorizing the continued separation from my child?

Until these foundational questions are answered with proper citation to authority, I cannot meaningfully participate in any discussion about allegations, as the proceeding lacks proper foundation.

I request these questions be answered for the record before we proceed further."

🎯 Why This Script Works:

1. Establishes you as the wronged party from the first sentence

2. Frames everything as verification, not argument

3. Asks in logical order - jurisdiction before substance

4. Uses precise legal terminology - "due process," "jurisdiction," "fundamental rights"

5. Ends with clear boundary - won't proceed until foundations established

6. Builds appeal record when they can't answer properly

🔄 Response Flow for Their Objections

If they say: "We don't have time for all these questions"

You respond: "Your Honor, jurisdiction is always the first question. If the court lacks jurisdiction, we save time by not proceeding improperly. This verification should take only moments if your authority is properly established."

If they say: "Your questions are irrelevant"

You respond: "With respect, the authority to adjudicate matters affecting fundamental rights is never irrelevant. These questions go to the very foundation of this proceeding. If they cannot be answered, that itself is relevant to the record."

If they say: "We'll get to that later"

You respond: "Jurisdiction cannot be addressed 'later' - it's threshold. I cannot knowingly participate in a proceeding without first verifying proper authority. Let the record reflect my request for jurisdictional verification was deferred."

If they refuse to answer:

You respond: "For the record, I note the refusal to provide verification of authority. I will proceed under protest and duress, reserving all rights and remedies for lack of jurisdiction and due process violations."

Remember: Every question should either (1) verify their authority, (2) demand definitions, (3) require evidence, or (4) expose procedural violations.

Your power comes from making THEM prove THEIR case. Never defend - always verify.

Practice these questions until they feel natural. Your calm, professional questioning will expose their lack of foundation.
