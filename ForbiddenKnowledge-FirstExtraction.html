Amnesty Coalition for Unity · Internal Protocol

The First Extraction

The architecture is nothing without the first person moved to safety. This is the step-by-step protocol. Lawful. Documented. Repeatable. Built for the Response Team Lead who will run it.

OPERATOR NOTE: This protocol is for the first extraction only. Once it is complete, it becomes the template for every extraction that follows. Every step is documented. Every dollar is tracked. Every person is protected. The rescued person's identity is never public. The route is never public. The code word is never written down.

01Before the Code Word

The Pre-Extraction Checklist

The Response Team must be ready before the first code word arrives. These are the things that must be in place.

  • Colombian attorney on retainer, with a signed engagement letter
  • At least one sanctuary host vetted, registered, and under hosting agreement
  • Extraction fund fully funded for one unit ($2,400)
  • Response Team Lead identified and reachable 24/7
  • Secondary identity verification channel established (a family member, a friend, a known contact)
  • Transport partners identified (lawful, insured, documented)
  • Colombian immigration requirements documented and current
  • Public ledger page live and auditable
  • Code word system tested with a dry run
  • Witness Lead ready to record the extraction (anonymized)

02The Code Word Arrives

STEP 1 · 0–15 MINUTES
Verify Identity

The Response Team Lead receives the code word. The first action is not to act. It is to verify. A code word can be coerced. A code word can be sent by someone else. The Lead contacts the member through a secondary channel—a pre-arranged family member, a known friend, a second phone number—and asks a question only the real member can answer.

If verification fails: The protocol stops. The Lead does not proceed. The Lead documents the failed verification and escalates to the trustees.

If verification succeeds: Proceed to Step 2.

STEP 2 · 15–45 MINUTES
Assess the Danger

The Lead asks three questions. The member answers. The Lead documents the answers.

  • Is the danger legal, physical, or medical?
  • Is the member currently safe, or in immediate danger?
  • Are there others with the member who need extraction (children, spouse, parent)?

If immediate danger: The Lead contacts local emergency services through the attorney, not directly. The attorney manages the local interaction.

If not immediate danger: Proceed to Step 3.

03The Legal and Sanctuary Coordination

STEP 3 · 1–2 HOURS
Contact the Colombian Attorney

The Legal Liaison contacts the Colombian attorney on retainer. The attorney reviews the member's situation and determines the lawful path: which visa, which documents, which timeline. The attorney does not promise a visa. The attorney identifies the lawful path and the requirements for that path.

Documentation required: Valid passport (or emergency travel document), birth certificate, any legal documents relevant to the danger, and the member's signed consent to representation.

STEP 4 · 2–4 HOURS
Confirm the Sanctuary Host

The Sanctuary Coordinator contacts the vetted host. The host confirms availability. The host confirms the duration. The host confirms the number of people they can receive. The host signs the hosting agreement if not already signed.

The host's identity is known to the fund. The host's identity is not public. The rescued person's identity is not public. The address is not public.

04The Extraction

STEP 5 · 24–72 HOURS
Arrange Transport

The Logistics Lead arranges lawful, documented, insured transport. The route is determined by the attorney based on the member's location and the visa requirements. The transport is booked in the member's name or in a name approved by the attorney. The member receives the itinerary through a secure channel.

No smuggling. No unlawful border crossings. No undocumented entry. The transport is lawful. The entry is lawful. The visa is lawful.

STEP 6 · 72 HOURS–2 WEEKS
Visa Application and Documentation

The attorney files the visa application. The fund pays the fees. The member provides the documents. The attorney manages the timeline. The member is kept informed at every stage through the secure channel.

If the visa is denied: The attorney identifies the reason and the appeal path. The fund continues to support the member while the appeal is pending. The extraction is delayed, not abandoned.

STEP 7 · ON ARRIVAL
Sanctuary Placement

The member arrives in Colombia. The Sanctuary Coordinator meets the member at the airport (or arranges a trusted representative to meet them). The member is transported to the host's home. The member receives the first month's stipend for food and necessities. The member is registered with the attorney for follow-up.

The member is now in sanctuary. The extraction is complete. The protocol continues with follow-up.

05After the Extraction

STEP 8 · 24 HOURS AFTER ARRIVAL
Record the Extraction (Anonymized)

The Witness Lead records the extraction on the public ledger. The record includes: the date, the number of people extracted, the cost, and the current status (in sanctuary, in process, pending). The record does not include: the name, the location, the host, the route, or the attorney.

STEP 9 · 30 DAYS AFTER ARRIVAL
Follow-Up and Integration

The Sanctuary Coordinator checks in with the member and the host. The attorney confirms the member's legal status. The fund disburses the second month's stipend. The member is introduced to the local PMA community. The member is offered the opportunity to become a member of the nation, if they choose.

STEP 10 · 90 DAYS AFTER ARRIVAL
Transition to Independence

The member's legal status is confirmed. The member's housing situation is assessed. The member's employment or income path is identified. The fund's support transitions from full stipend to partial stipend to self-sufficiency. The member is now a full participant in the nation, if they choose.

06The Warnings

WARNING 1: The code word can be coerced. Never act on a code word without secondary verification. A coerced code word is worse than no code word, because it puts the member and the team at risk.
WARNING 2: The route is not public. The route is determined by the attorney for each extraction. It is never shared with the public ledger. It is never shared with anyone outside the Response Team.
WARNING 3: The host is not public. The host's identity is known to the fund and the attorney. It is never public. If the host is compromised, the extraction fails and the member is at risk.
WARNING 4: The fund does not break laws. The fund does not smuggle. The fund does not pay bribes. The fund does not use false documents. The fund uses the lawful path, the lawful visa, and the lawful entry. If the lawful path is not available, the extraction is delayed, not abandoned.

07What Happens Next

Once the first extraction is complete, the protocol becomes the template. The second extraction is faster. The third is faster still. The fund's cost per extraction drops as the team gains experience. The public ledger shows the first person helped. The story is told (anonymized). The nation is real.

This is the work. Not the terminal. Not the dashboard. Not the architecture. The work is the first person who sleeps safely because the nation acted.
