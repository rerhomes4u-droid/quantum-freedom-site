The Breath Practice

Take a single breath. Hold it. Release it. In the pause between exhale and inhale, notice that there is no "you" noticing. There is just the pause. That pause is the zero point.

Practice 01 · The Breath
00:00

Follow the breath. The app will guide you. One cycle: inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. Then begin again.

What to notice

When the breath is done, sit for a moment. Ask yourself: Who was breathing? The answer is not "me." The answer is not "the body." The answer is not "the mind." The answer is the space in which the breath appeared. That space is the zero point.

You do not need to name it. You do not need to grasp it. It is already here. It has always been here. It is the only thing that has never left.

The Silence Practice

Sit in silence. Not the silence of a quiet room. The silence of the mind. The silence that is underneath the thoughts. The silence that is always there, even when the thoughts are loud. The silence is the zero point.

Practice 02 · The Silence
05:00

Choose a duration. Sit. Do not try to stop the thoughts. Do not try to empty the mind. Just sit. The silence is not the absence of thoughts. The silence is the space in which the thoughts appear.

What to notice

When the timer ends, sit for a moment. Ask yourself: What was there before the first thought? The answer is the zero point. It was there before the thought. It was there during the thought. It was there after the thought. It is here now.

The Thought Watcher

Watch your thoughts. Do not try to stop them. Do not try to change them. Just watch them. Every time a thought appears, press the button. At the end of one minute, the app will show you the dots. The dots are the thoughts you noticed. The one who noticed them is the zero point.

The Chapter — The Forbidden Question

I am going to ask you a question. And I need you to not look away. What if you are already dead?

Not metaphorically. Not spiritually. Not in some poetic sense. What if the "you" that is reading these words—the one that remembers a childhood, that fears a future, that carries a name, that has a story—what if that "you" is a ghost? A narrative. A story told by a brain that cannot tell the difference between memory and imagination. A collection of electrical patterns that have already dissolved and reformed a million times since you started reading this chapter.

The person you think you were yesterday never existed except as an electrical pattern that has already died.

You are not a being having an experience. You are experience being momentarily mistaken for a being.

Think about it. When you were five years old, you had a name, a face, a set of fears, a set of desires. Where is that five-year-old now? Dead. Gone. Dissolved into the past. The cells that made up that body have been replaced. The memories have been rewritten. The fears have evolved. The name is the same, but the person is not.

You have died a thousand times. You died when you were ten, when you were fifteen, when you were twenty-five. You died every time you changed, every time you grew, every time you let go of something that used to define you. And yet—here you are. Still reading. Still aware. Still alive.

Fear of death is the root of all other fears. Fear of loss. Fear of pain. Fear of insignificance. Fear of being forgotten. Every fear you have ever had is a child of the fear of death.

But what if death is not the enemy? What if death is not an ending? If you have already died a thousand times—each night in sleep, each moment of forgetting, each time you shed an old identity—and yet awareness remains, then death was never the enemy. The enemy was the story that death is an ending. Death is a punctuation mark. The sentence continues.

The master key: If you are already dead, what is there to lose? Exactly. That question is the master key to every lock. It is the key to the prison of fear. It is the key to the cage of identity. It is the key to the spell of scarcity, the spell of debt, the spell of submission. If you are already dead, you cannot be killed. If you are already dead, you cannot be threatened. If you are already dead, you cannot be controlled. The system cannot threaten a ghost. The system cannot imprison a memory. The system cannot tax a pattern of electricity.

The sacredness of nothing: Does this mean nothing matters? It means everything matters more. If this is the only awareness you will ever have of this precise configuration of atoms, this particular slant of light, this specific ache in your knee—then the ache is sacred. The light is a miracle. The atom is a universe. The realization that nothing lasts is not a reason for nihilism; it is the only possible foundation for reverence.

Lie down in the dark. Do not sleep. Feel your breath. Notice that you are not controlling it—it is happening to you. Notice that you are not thinking thoughts—they are happening to you. Notice that the "you" noticing is also happening to you. There is no captain. There is only the ship, sailing itself.

Surrender to that. Not as a philosophy. As a sensation. When the sensation of being a separate self dissolves, even for a second, you will have tasted the truth that every mystic, every prophet, every fool who ever lived was trying to describe. It cannot be described. But it can be tasted. Taste it.

The dead do not fear. They also do not hope. They simply are. You are already there. You just keep forgetting. Remember. Then forget again. Then remember again. That dance—not the destination—is the only heaven that exists.
