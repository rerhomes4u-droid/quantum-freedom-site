A Voice for Survivors

Who Randy Mills was

Randy Mills was a former journalist and political operative who became the highest-profile advocate of "ritual sex abuse awareness" in Montana during the early 1990s. He testified before the Montana state legislature in 1993, sharing his recovered memories of horrific ritual abuse from early childhood.

His testimony led Montana to enact a standalone statute criminalizing "ritual child abuse" — a law that remains on the books to this day, though no prosecutions were ever brought under it. Mills claimed his abuse was so severe that his personality had shattered into more than 100 identifiable pieces, a condition known as Dissociative Identity Disorder (formerly Multiple Personality Disorder).

The Controversy

Recovered memories and the Satanic Panic

🚨 The Satanic Panic Era

Mills' testimony came during the "Satanic Panic" of the 1980s and 1990s, a period of moral panic involving widespread allegations of ritual abuse. Many of these allegations were later found to be based on recovered memories that were influenced by therapists and suggestive questioning techniques.

🧠 Recovered Memory Therapy

Mills' memories emerged during therapy, a process that skeptics argue can create false memories through suggestion and influence. The reliability of recovered memories has been extensively debated in the psychological and legal communities.

Contested Practice

📰 The Ms. Magazine Article

Mills cited a Ms. Magazine article contrived by Gloria Steinem as validation of his memories. This article was later criticized for contributing to the moral panic and for presenting dubious claims as fact.

Questioned Source

❓ The Question of Truth

Some argue that Mills genuinely believed his memories were real, while others contend they were confabulated. The truth may never be fully known, but the debate itself highlights the importance of careful investigation and the dangers of both dismissing survivors and accepting unverified claims.
