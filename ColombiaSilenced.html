THE NAMED

These are some of the 112. They are not statistics. They are human beings. They had names. They had families. They had dreams. They had courage.

Arnobio Pérez

Community Leader, Pereira

President of the Junta de Acción Comunal in the Zea neighborhood. Killed by armed men while standing on a street. He fought for the people of his community until his last breath [citation:1].

Héctor Bastidas Ibarra

Notary, Túquerres, Nariño

Lawyer, notary, former councilman, and candidate for mayor. He was number 100. His legacy of community service and peacebuilding will not be forgotten [citation:8].

Mateo Pérez Rueda

Journalist, Yarumal, Antioquia

23 years old. Founder of "El Confidente." He covered corruption, security, and local politics. He was kidnapped and murdered for it. He was the first journalist killed in 2026 [citation:4].

Cristian Herrera

Journalist, Cúcuta

48 years old. He had protection measures because of threats. He investigated corruption. He was shot dead on June 6, 2026. His protection detail was not with him that day [citation:3].

Julián Andrés Hincapié

Community Communicator, Anorí, Antioquia

He disappeared on September 10, 2026. His body was found 22 days later. He was a communicator with the Campesino Association of Northern Antioquia. He was the third journalist killed in 2026 [citation:5].

Freiman David Velásquez

Youth Leader, Catatumbo

President of the Departmental Youth Council of Norte de Santander. Member of ASUNCAT and the Patriotic Union. He was ambushed by armed men. Six people were killed in that attack, including two bodyguards [citation:25].

José Deifer Giraldo & Yerlín Andrey Giraldo

Peace Signatories, Cajibío, Cauca

Father and son. Both were peace signatories. Both searched for disappeared persons. Both were executed by armed men on August 28, 2026 [citation:27].

Pedro Luis González "Genaro"

Peace Signatory, Campoalegre, Huila

Former FARC combatant who laid down his arms and signed the peace agreement. He was shot at his home. He was one of 10 peace signatories killed in 2026 [citation:36].

These are only eight of the 112. The others include campesinos, indigenous leaders, Afro-Colombian defenders, women leaders, and young people. They all had names. We simply do not have the space to list them all. But they are not forgotten.

🔥 THE BURNING

The land is being burned. Not by accident. Not by nature. By design.

In Tolima, there are 650 requests for mining titles covering 770,000 hectares of land. Of those, 311 are for fracking [citation:7].

Senator Kevin Gómez Paz has documented a pattern: land that has been proposed for protection as a campesino reserve, or as an Area of Protection for Food Production, mysteriously catches fire. After the fire, the protection is lifted. After the protection is lifted, the mining title is approved. After the mining title is approved, the fracking begins [citation:7].

"What a coincidence," said former Minister of Mines and Energy Andrés Camacho. "Fires in lands where mining and oil projects want to be developed. Meanwhile, agricultural protection figures are eliminated. What coincidences..." [citation:7].

Senator Iván Cepeda called it what it is: "capitalism of disaster" — the deliberate destruction of land and life for profit [citation:37].

And while the land burns, the leaders who defend it are killed. The campesinos who refuse to sell are killed. The journalists who report on it are killed. The peace signatories who try to build a different future are killed.

This is not chaos. This is a system.
