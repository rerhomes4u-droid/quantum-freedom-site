✍️ UCC §3-401 – Signature: Any Mark, Symbol, Trade Name

“A signature may be made (i) manually or by means of a device or machine, and (ii) by the use of any name, including a trade or assumed name, or by a word, mark, or symbol executed or adopted by a person with present intention to authenticate a writing.”

“Your ‘legal signature’ is whatever you intend it to be. A squiggle. A stamp. ‘X’. Even your printed name. The idea of a ‘legal signature’ separate from your regular signature is made‑up nonsense.”

👤 UCC §3-402 – Signature by Representative (You + The ALL CAPS Name)

“If a person acting or purporting to act as representative signs an instrument by signing either the name of the represented person or the name of the signer, the represented person is bound to the same extent as if the signature were on a simple contract.”

⚠️ UCC §3-403 – Unauthorized Signature (Ratification)

“Unless otherwise provided, an unauthorized signature is ineffective except as the signature of the unauthorized signer in favor of a person who in good faith pays the instrument or takes it for value. An unauthorized signature may be ratified for all purposes.”

“If you signed something without authority (e.g., as ‘president’ of a company you don’t control), you can later ratify it – or use it to show the other party accepted an unauthorized signature.” This is a defense weapon: if the bank accepted a signature without proper authority, they bear the loss.

🎭 UCC §3-404 – Impostors & Fictitious Payees (The ALL CAPS Trap)

“If an impostor induces the issuer to issue an instrument to the impostor by impersonating the payee, an endorsement by any person in the name of the payee is effective.”
“If the person identified as payee is a fictitious person, an endorsement by any person in the name of the payee is effective.”

“The ALL CAPS name is a fictitious person (a legal entity, not a natural person). Therefore, any endorsement in that name is effective – including the bank’s endorsement ‘Pay to the order of Bank of America.’ That’s how they steal your note.” But if you are the representative, you can counter‑endorse.

🏢 UCC §3-405 – Employer’s Responsibility (Fraudulent Endorsement by Employee)

“If an employer entrusts an employee with responsibility with respect to an instrument, and the employee makes a fraudulent endorsement, the endorsement is effective as the endorsement of the person to whom the instrument is payable.”

“This is why banks win when a bookkeeper steals checks. The employer is liable because they entrusted the employee. Apply this to yourself: if you ‘entrusted’ the bank (by giving them blank endorsement), they have responsibility.”

⚠️ UCC §3-406 – Negligence Contributing to Forgery

“A person who fails to exercise ordinary care substantially contributing to an alteration or forged signature is precluded from asserting the alteration or forgery against a person who in good faith pays the instrument.”

“If you were sloppy with your blank endorsement, you can’t later complain that the bank altered it. That’s why you must use special endorsements and ‘without recourse.’ Ordinary care is the standard.”

✍️ UCC §3-407 – Alteration of Instrument

“Alteration discharges a party whose obligation is affected by the alteration unless the party assents or is precluded from asserting it. No other alteration discharges a party; the instrument may be enforced according to its original terms.”

“If someone changes the amount on your endorsed note without your consent, that alteration discharges you. You are no longer liable for the altered amount.” Use this when banks change due dates or add fees.

🔏 UCC §3-409 – Acceptance of Draft (The Delivery Theory)

“Acceptance means the drawee’s signed agreement to pay a draft as presented. It must be written on the draft and may consist of the drawee’s signature alone. Acceptance may be made at any time and becomes effective when notification is given or the accepted draft is delivered.”

⚔️ UCC §3-415 – Obligation of Endorser (Without Recourse = No Liability)

“If an instrument is dishonored, an endorser is obligated to pay the amount due. If an endorsement states that it is made ‘without recourse’ or otherwise disclaims liability, the endorser is not liable.”

“This is the nuclear weapon. When you endorse a check, a bill, or a mortgage note with ‘Without recourse,’ you have zero liability. They cannot come after you. It’s the ultimate ‘get out of jail free’ card.”
