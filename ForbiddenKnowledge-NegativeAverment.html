THE NOTICE

THE NOTICE

A negative averment is a statement of what the record does not show. It is a statement of what the other side has not alleged. It is a statement of what the evidence does not contain.

It is a tool that puts the other side on notice. It forces them to either produce the evidence or admit the absence of evidence. It is a way of saying: "I have looked. I have searched. And this is what is missing."

"If they have the evidence, let them produce it. If they don't, let the record show they don't."

▣ WHAT A NEGATIVE AVERMENT IS
DEFINITION
A negative averment is a statement that a particular fact is not true, or that a particular thing does not exist, or that a particular event did not happen. It shifts the burden to the other side to prove that the fact is true, the thing does exist, or the event did happen.
WHY IT MATTERS
In most cases, the person making a claim has the burden of proving it. But if you are the one being accused, a negative averment forces the accuser to put up or shut up. It says: "You claim I did X. I say I did not. Show your evidence."
THE TRAP
If the other side cannot produce the evidence, the negative averment stands. If they produce false evidence, you can prove it is false. Either way, you win.
▣ EXAMPLES
"The record contains no evidence that [Person] ever contacted [Other Person] regarding [Subject]."
"The record contains no document signed by [Person] authorizing [Action]."
"The record contains no court order authorizing the removal of [Child] from [Parent]."
"The record contains no evidence that [Agency] conducted any investigation into [Allegation]."
"The record contains no evidence that [Person] was ever charged with or convicted of [Crime]."
"The record contains no evidence that [Person] ever provided [Document] to [Agency]."
"The record contains no evidence that [Agency] ever provided [Document] to [Person], despite repeated written requests."
"The record contains no evidence that [Person] was ever given notice of [Proceeding]."
"The record contains no evidence that [Person] was ever given an opportunity to be heard before [Action] was taken."
"The record contains no evidence that [Agency] ever interviewed [Person] about [Allegation]."
▣ THE STRATEGY
STEP 1
Identify what is missing. What evidence should exist? What document should exist? What record should exist? What investigation should have happened? If it is not in the record, it is a negative averment waiting to be made.
STEP 2
Verify the absence. Check the record. Check the docket. Check the discovery responses. Make sure the evidence really is not there. You don't want to file a negative averment and then have the other side produce the document you said did not exist.
STEP 3
Draft the averment. Each averment should be a single, specific statement. "The record contains no evidence that X." Not "The other side is lying." Not "They are hiding something." Just: this is what the record shows. This is what the record does not show.
STEP 4
File the notice. Serve it on the other side. File it with the court. Put it in the record. Now the other side knows you are watching. And they know that if they cannot produce the evidence, the negative averment stands.
STEP 5
Follow up. If the other side produces the evidence, you were wrong. That is fine. You learned something. If they do not produce it, the negative averment stands. That is your win.

▣ THE PRO TIPS
TIP 1
Be specific. "The record contains no evidence that Jessica Hudson ever contacted Nolvia" is better than "The record contains no evidence that DSS did its job." Specific averments are harder to answer. Specific averments are harder to explain away.
TIP 2
Be verifiable. If you say the record contains no evidence of X, make sure you have actually checked the record. If the other side produces the evidence, you look careless. If you are right, you look careful.
TIP 3
Be patient. A negative averment does not have to be responded to immediately. It puts the other side on notice. The notice is the point. The other side has to live with the fact that the record says what it says.
TIP 4
Be organized. Number each averment. Reference the record. Reference the specific document or the specific absence of a document. The more organized you are, the harder it is to dismiss you.
TIP 5
Be strategic. Not every absence is worth a negative averment. Choose the ones that matter. Choose the ones that would change the outcome if the other side cannot answer them.
▣ THE CLOSING
Negative averments are the tool that puts the other side on notice. They are the record of what is missing. They are the demand for evidence.
Carolina filed them. She put the system on notice. She said: "This is what the record shows. This is what the record does not show." Now the other side has to answer. Or not. Either way, the record is clear.
Use them. The truth shall set you free. But first, it will make you put the other side on notice.
